Privacy

Last updated: 28 September 2026

This page explains what happens to your data when you play napoland at www.napoland.com. napoland is open source and made by its community, so you can also read exactly what the game does in its source code. Copies of napoland that other people run on their own servers are theirs, with their own rules.

The short version

  • We collect only what the game needs to run.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking and no cookies. We never sell your data or share it for marketing.
  • You can play without signing in, as a guest. Nothing then says who you are, and a guest who does not play for 30 days is deleted. So is a character made before sign-in existed that nobody has signed in with: it counts as a guest.
  • Your email address (and, if you sign in with Google or Apple, your name) is used only to sign you in. Other players never see it.
  • Other players see your character's name. Your neighbors on your street in the game also see it on your cabin, and whether you are at home, and can walk into your cabin to see the furniture you made and your trophy shelf, whether you are home or not, unless you turn those off in the game.
  • Some of the steps your character walks out in the wilds may be shown for a day to someone alone there, as a see-through figure in your character's color, never with its name. Our server keeps them only in its memory.
  • Private messages to a friend are kept only until they read them. Chat is not kept at all. Who thanked whom in the game is kept for 7 days.
  • Our servers never store your IP address.
  • You can ask us to delete everything, at any time.

What we collect, and why

To sign you in

You sign in with your email address and a 6-digit code sent to it, or with your Google or Apple account. Signing in is handled by Supabase, which keeps your email address and a record of your sign-ins (when, and from which IP address and browser) to send the codes and keep accounts safe. Your browser talks to Supabase directly for this. Our game server then checks your sign-in and keeps only the random id Supabase gave your account, never your email address. We never store your codes or sign-in tokens.

If you sign in with Google or Apple, you do it on Google's or Apple's own page, and that company then tells Supabase your email address and your name as your account gives it (Google also sends your profile picture). Apple can give a private relay address instead of your real one, which forwards to it. Supabase keeps these with your account; our game server still keeps only Supabase's random id for it. Google or Apple also learns that you signed in to napoland, and their own privacy policies cover what happens on their pages.

Playing as a guest

You can play without signing in, as a guest. Your character is then kept on our server like anyone's (everything under "Your character" below), and your browser keeps a login token for it in its local storage; our server keeps only a scrambled copy of that token. Nothing about a guest says who you are: no email address and no account. A guest cannot chat or have friends, and other players can still block or report a guest. If your browser's data is cleared, the token goes with it, and so does the way back to your character. A guest who does not play for 30 days is deleted, with its pile, arrows, thanks and what it left in crates. Signing in keeps your character: from then on it belongs to your account.

A character made before sign-in existed, played with the login token its browser keeps, counts as a guest until someone signs in with it on that browser: if it is not played for 30 days, it is deleted like a guest. This rule deletes nobody before it has been in the game for 30 days, so everyone who plays can read it there first.

Your character

Its name and color, where it is in the world, its energy, how wet it is, what it wears (and how worn each piece is), the outfit it wears over it, if any, past level 20 the merits it spent, the jacket patterns and name tag badges it bought with them and the ones it wears, what is in its bag and in its stash at home (and what it took out of the stash), the tools it owns (for now, its paper maps), its XP and the rest it saved up while you were away (which makes stashing count double for a while), what counts toward its feats (among them how many thanks it received), how many pieces of gear it made and how often it collapsed or was caught by a surge (and which of the townspeople's words about those it heard), how far it is in the story (the latest chapter it reached), which pages of its field notes opened and which of their questions it saw answered (reading a sign or a paper tells our server where it stands, never anything else), which of the notes people left out there it read and which keepsakes it brought home, for the daily parcels (signed in only) whether it had its welcome parcel, the day of its last parcel and which days of that week it played, the arrows it paints on the ground, what it leaves in a crate for whoever comes next (and when), the pile of things it leaves when it collapses (with the last steps it walked before, which others see as a pale figure walking back to the pile), the furniture it made for its cabin and until when it is cozy from its own fire, its best trips (the farthest out, the longest and the most XP brought home: how far, how long and how much, never the route), the street and the lot its cabin stands on, whether you keep its name off its door, whether you let your neighbors into its cabin, whether you read the letter about its street, while it is new which of its first steps it is on (to town, out of town to pick something up, and home to the chest; forgotten once it took the last), and when it was created and last played. The game cannot run without these. Other players on the same map see your character's name and color, what it wears (its gear, or the outfit over it, and the pattern on its jacket), the badge on its name tag, where it walks, whose pile or arrow is whose, who fed a fire last, and who left what in a crate, and when; in your own cabin, only the neighbors you let in see you and what is in it (below). When your character runs out of energy out in the wilds and lies down waiting for help, the players on that map see it lying there, and their local chat says so once, with its name and the nearest landmark, walking ("Ana is down by the pond"), never where exactly; players who blocked you do not get that line. Like the rest of chat, the line is not kept.

Your cabin stands on a street of about 30 neighbors (guests too). Everyone on your street sees your character's name on the plate by your cabin's door, online or not, and sees its window lit while you are in the game and at home in your cabin. A neighbor who knocks at your door is told whether you are home, and if you are, you read their character's name; someone you block knocks without you hearing it. Knocks are not kept. At your own door the game offers to move your cabin next to a friend whose street has room, naming those friends to you alone.

You can keep your name and whether you are home to yourself, guest or not: in the game's menu, under Friends, turn off "Show my name on my door and when I am home". Then your plate says only "A resident", your window never lights for anyone, and a neighbor who knocks is told that nobody answers, unless they are your friend; you still read who knocked while you are home. It is on until you turn it off, and we keep the setting with your character. The first time you come home, a letter in the game says what your neighbors see and where to change it; we keep that you read it, so it comes once.

Your neighbors can also walk into your cabin, whether you are home or not, to see how you made it yours: the furniture you made for it, and what stands on your trophy shelf once you made one (each charm and each piece of anomalous gear in your stash, once; while you are away, as they were when you last left the game). Nothing else of your stash, your bag or your chest is shown, and nobody but you opens your chest or works at your workbench. If you are home, you read the character name of whoever comes in; that is not kept. Someone you block never comes in, nor does someone who blocks you, and blocking someone who is inside sends them out.

You can keep your cabin to your friends, guest or not: in the game's menu, under Friends, turn off "Let my neighbors come into my cabin". Then only your friends come in, as they do when you keep your name off your door; whoever else is inside when you turn it off walks out. It is on until you turn it off, and we keep the setting with your character.

Steps others glimpse

When your character walks at least 20 steps out in the wilds and the walk ends at a find it picks up, a fire it feeds or the way home, our server keeps the last of those steps (up to 40: the tiles, in order), with your character's color and which character took them, so that it never shows you your own. A player who is then alone in that area may see a see-through figure in your character's color walk those steps. Nothing shown with it says whose they were: no name and no id. The server keeps these steps only in its memory, never in the database or in a log: a day at most, fewer when many people walk there (it keeps a few for each area and forgets the oldest first), and a restart forgets them all.

First finders

If your character is the first on our server to find one of the secrets out there (to read a note someone left, or to pick up a keepsake), we keep its name with that secret, with the game's day and when, and show it to everyone: in a one-line message to everyone playing at that moment, on the notice board in town, and in the journal of whoever finds it after. Only the first finder of each secret is kept, a guest's too, and it is shown to everyone, including players you block.

Thanks

You can thank another player for a fire they fed or an arrow they painted, taking something another player left in a crate thanks them, and a player who gets you back on your feet when you were down out in the wilds is thanked by you as you get up. A thanks carries no words. We keep who thanked whom, when, and for what (which fire or arrow, what was left in which crate, or who was got back up, and where), for 7 days: so that each player thanks each other player at most once a day, and so that the player you thanked can read who thanked them, and for what, when they next come home in the game. After 7 days it is deleted. A fire remembers the names of the last three players who fed it only in the server's memory, until the server restarts; players on its map see them, as they see whose arrow is whose. If you block someone, their thanks never reach you.

Friends and private messages

Who your friends are, the friend requests you sent and received, who you block, and whether you take friend requests and trade requests at all. Your friends see when you are online and on which map. A private message you send a friend is kept on our server, with when you sent it, only until they read it: reading it deletes it. The conversation you see stays in your browser only until you close the game.

A trade with a friend is kept only while it lasts, in our server's memory: whom it is with and what each of you puts in. Once it is done, what changed hands is simply in your bags, like anything else you carry; once it is off, nothing of it is kept.

Chat

What you say in chat goes to the players who can hear it: everyone online for world chat, the players near you for local chat (it also shows over your head). Our server passes it on and keeps none of it: not in the database and not in any log. Your game shows what you heard until you close it. Only signed-in players can talk, and some words are masked before anyone reads them. If you block someone, you no longer hear them.

A call (the short note your character sings when you hold B) goes the same way to the players on your map within 25 tiles, with the tile you stood on, and our server keeps none of it either. Guests can call too: a call has no words. If you block someone, you no longer hear their calls.

If you report someone, we keep who reported whom, the reason you chose, when, and the last thing they wrote to you as your game showed it, so that the people who look after napoland (people, not a program) can read it and deal with it. From chat, what you report is the last thing they wrote to you or said where you could hear it. The person you report is not told who reported them. The server log notes that a report was made, with both characters' ids and the reason, never what was written.

While you play

While you are connected, the game server uses your IP address, in memory only, to limit how many connections and new characters one address can make. That protects the game from abuse. The address is never written down: not in the database and not in any log. Our web server keeps no record of your visits.

Server logs

The game server writes down technical events, such as a character being created, joining, leaving or collapsing, with the character's id and name, and errors, so that we can keep the game running and fix problems. These logs never contain email addresses, IP addresses, codes or tokens, and they are deleted after 30 days.

In your browser

The game keeps your sign-in session in your browser's local storage so you stay signed in (it includes your email address, and with Google or Apple your name), and, for the few minutes a sign-in takes, what it needs to finish: which address a code was sent to, or that you chose Google or Apple, with a one-time key that only this browser holds. If you play as a guest, or made your character before sign-in existed, your browser keeps its login token there; our server keeps only a scrambled copy of it. It also keeps your sound volume there, whether you turned the sound off, whether your radio is on, and how many calls you have sung (after the first few, the calls are shown without their words). None of this leaves your device, except to sign you in. We use no cookies and no trackers. Apart from signing in, everything the game loads, fonts included, comes from our own server.

Why we are allowed to

  • To give you the game you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR): your account, your character, your friends and your messages, and everything needed to play.
  • Our legitimate interest in keeping the game safe and running (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR): the limits per address, blocks and reports, the logs and the backups.

We do nothing else with your data: no profiling, no advertising, no automated decisions about you.

How long we keep it

  • Your character once you signed in with it, and your sign-in account at Supabase with its record of your sign-ins: until you ask us to delete them.
  • A guest's character, and a character made before sign-in existed that nobody has signed in with (it counts as a guest): until it has not been played for 30 days, when it is deleted with its pile, arrows, thanks, what it left in crates and its first finds, but never before the rule has been in the game for 30 days. Signing in keeps it, as above.
  • A pile, with the last steps before the collapse: an hour. An arrow you paint: a day (up to a week, with the Good neighbor feat).
  • The last steps of a walk out in the wilds, which others alone there may glimpse: a day at most, in our server's memory only.
  • Who thanked whom, when and for what: 7 days.
  • Something you leave in a crate for whoever comes next, with your character's name and when: until someone takes it.
  • Being the first to find a secret, with your character's name and the day: as long as your character exists.
  • A private message: until your friend reads it. Friends, requests and blocks: until you remove them.
  • A report: until we have dealt with it.
  • Server logs: 30 days.
  • Nightly database backups: 30 days. Server disk snapshots: 7 days.

When you ask us to delete your data, we delete your character, its pile and arrows, the thanks it gave and received, what it left in crates, the secrets it found first, its friends, requests, blocks and unread messages (to and from you), your sign-in account and its record of sign-ins. The copies inside backups disappear by themselves within 30 days after that.

Your rights

You can ask us to show you your data, correct it, delete it, limit what we do with it, or give it to you in a form you can take elsewhere, and you can object to how we use it. Write to support@neuramare.com from the email address you sign in with, so that we know it is you (as a guest, sign in first: nothing else tells us a character is yours). If Apple hides your email from us, tell us the relay address it gave napoland: we answer there. We answer within 30 days.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC); in the EU or the EEA, the authority of the country where you live.

Children

napoland is not meant for children under 13, and they should not sign in. If you are younger than the age at which your country lets you agree to this on your own (up to 16 in parts of the EU), ask a parent or guardian first. If we learn that a child under 13 has an account, we delete it.

Security

Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Our server accepts only web traffic, and only the publisher can manage it. Login tokens are kept only as scrambled copies, and the whole game is open for anyone to review.

Changes

When this page changes, the date at the top changes too. If a change affects how your data is used, we will tell players in the game before it takes effect.
