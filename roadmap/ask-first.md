title: Ask before using anything up, then say what it did
status: done
order: 25
area: controls
---

Anything you do that uses up what you carry or keep asks first, like Pokémon's YES/NO: burning something in a fire, giving the Old Stone a shard, drinking a thermos, lighting a flare, crushing a glowcap for a mark, looking closely at a strange object, making or mending gear, and throwing something away. The question comes in the text box the townspeople speak in ("Feed the fire resin? ×1", "Look closely at the strange object? It will be used up."), with YES already chosen, so it costs one more press. Where more than one can go at once (a fire, the Old Stone), it also asks how many, as Pokémon does when you toss things. B, NO or a tap outside the box cancels, and nothing is spent; while it asks, the joystick moves between YES and NO and sets how many.

Say yes, and the same box says what it did, from the server's answer, never guessed: "The fire takes 3 resin. It will burn 18 more minutes." "It turns out to be a hollow feather. While it is in your bag, what you carry feels lighter." "The Old Stone takes the shard: 13 of 20." It closes with any press or by itself after a few seconds. The short words that float over your head for these today go, so there is one place to read it.

Everything added later that uses things up asks the same way: opening a lockbox, upgrading gear, cooking, giving to the woods' works, the research desk, Walt's swaps, making furniture, leaving something in a crate, spending merits. Only a trade asks nothing more, since both sides already confirm it. When the game already knows something cannot happen (nothing to burn, a fire someone else keeps going, a strange object out in the dark), it says why instead of asking.

Why: what took a trip to bring home should never go by accident, and a player should always learn what their choice did: what a charm does, how long a fire will burn, how close the Old Stone is to waking. It is one rule with [gear-details](gear-details.md): a tap looks, an action is a second step, and an action that uses something up asks first.
