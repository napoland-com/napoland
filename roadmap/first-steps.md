A new character is shown the loop in three small steps, one at a time, on a small card at the foot of the status panel: take NAPO's teleport in your cabin to town; go out of town and pick up something you find in the woods; take the teleport by the notice board home and put what you found in the chest. Each step counts only in its turn, and the card moves on the moment it is taken; after the last, the text box says once that those are the basics. Nothing is timed, failed or given for them: they only show the way, beside the first chapter, Mira's hint and the first goal ([first-day](first-day.md)).

For it, NAPO's teleport goes both ways: A at the one by the notice board sets you down at home, in front of the one in your own cabin, and that is coming home as walking in is (the trip ends, and its card says how it went). A at either asks first, since it takes you somewhere else. And it looks more like NAPO's: a steel arch on a pad rimmed in yellow, its humming rock floating and turning in it over a glow that breathes, and a ring of light spreading over the pad.

Why: a new player learns the loop by doing it once, in a couple of minutes (leave home, gather, come back), and does not have to find the long way home from town on the first trip. A way back as quick as the way out keeps trips short now that home is a walk from town, and asking first keeps a stray A from carrying anyone off.

Built: the three steps, kept with the character and saved as each is taken, for characters made since; the card on the status panel and the note after the last; the teleport home from town, asking first both ways; its new look.
