Stonebrook's south road leads somewhere now: the South Road, a second region of the wilds (depth 1, bigger than the Near Woods), through what NAPO left behind. The NAPO Bunker, where Ruth keeps the fire going so it never goes out; the camp the leavers made; the cars on the shoulders; the NAPO Research Station, with the NAPO Laboratory (Vera is still listening there), the dormitory and the stores; the NAPO Tower with its shed and the panel that works it; the sinks, NAPO's field site, and the checkpoint on the quarantine line, where the road is closed. NAPO's buildings are concrete, rimmed in NAPO yellow and concrete inside too; its warning signs are yellow; its logs, its radio and its panel are read like signs. Back in town, Tom sits by the fire in Stonebrook Lodge, and a signpost points the way. It never surges and nothing watches from its trees: the danger is how far it goes (the checkpoint is about 90 steps out).

Why: the story ([the design](../docs/DESIGN.md#the-story-napo-and-napoland)) needed a place in the world, told by what stands there and by the people who stayed, never by a mission. It is a second way out of town that teaches the same loop, and it makes the world bigger without moving anything players already know (one fixed world you learn).
