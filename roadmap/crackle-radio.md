An old NAPO field radio, rewired at the workbench at home from 2 copper wire and 1 scrap: a tool that sits in the bag's header for good, and its button turns it on and off (a small lamp glows while it is on). Out in the wilds it crackles louder the closer you are to a shard, a strange object or, on aurora nights, copper wire (loud within 6 tiles, faint within 15). Elsewhere it gives only the hum, which is everywhere; on each of the Tower's pulses it gives one burst, and in a storm it goes silent. It never shows a direction or a place: you walk and listen, hotter and colder.

Why: searching deep becomes a skill instead of a stroll, with no marker on screen. Pacific Drive's scanners.
