# napoland: architecture

How the game is built, and why it is built that way. The main constraint: Claude builds, tests, play-tests, releases and maintains every part, so everything is text in one repository, one language (TypeScript) runs everywhere, and every check is a command.

## Pieces

```
 phone / desktop browser                       one server (Docker)
 +---------------------------+   WebSocket    +-------------------------------+     +------------+
 | apps/client               |  /ws (JSON)    | apps/server                   |     | PostgreSQL |
 |  three.js world  (view/)  | <------------> |  net.ts    sessions, limits   | --> | players    |
 |  HUD + controls  (hud.ts) |                |  world.ts  rules, no I/O      |     +------------+
 |  prediction      (game.ts)|   HTTP         |  http.ts   /health, client,   |
 |  sign-in      (signin.ts) | <------------> |            /auth-config       |
 +---------------------------+                |  auth.ts   who is signing in  |
               |                              |  storage.ts memory | postgres |
               | email code, Google, Apple    +-------------------------------+
               v                                              |
 +---------------------------+   its public keys (JWKS)       |
 | Supabase Auth             | <------------------------------+
 +---------------------------+
            both import packages/shared: maps, movement and energy rules, protocol
```

- **packages/shared** is the contract: `TileMap` (walkable tiles, exits, lamp light and the distance home, from the map data), movement (`STEP_MS`, `findPath`), energy (`energyRate`), the protocol (zod schemas for every client message, types for every server message, how to sign in) and content validation. Client and server can never disagree about the rules because they run the same code.
- **apps/server** owns the world. `world.ts` holds the rules as plain functions of state and time (easy to test, replayable); `net.ts` turns WebSocket messages into world calls and world events into messages; `auth.ts` checks who is signing in; `storage.ts` keeps players in PostgreSQL (or in memory for tests and quick development).
- **apps/client** draws the world and sends intentions. It predicts your own steps so walking feels instant, and animates everyone else from the server's reports.
- **Supabase Auth** only proves who someone is (an email and a code, or a Google or Apple account). The game keeps its own players and everything they own in its own database.

## Movement: authoritative server, predicted client

- The world is a grid. A step moves one tile in 200 ms (`STEP_MS`), one speed for everyone.
- The client sends `{t:'step', dir, seq}`. The server accepts it when the player's previous step has finished (40 ms tolerance for network jitter) and the target tile is walkable; early steps wait in a queue of two. On average nobody can walk faster than one tile per 200 ms, however fast they send.
- Accepted: everyone gets `{t:'step', id, x, y, dir}`; the mover also gets its `seq` back. Refused: the mover gets `{t:'reject', seq, x, y, dir}` with the real position and snaps back.
- The client walks immediately (prediction) and stays at most two steps ahead of the server's confirmations. Other players are drawn sliding from where they are to the reported tile over one step's time.
- Turning in place sends `{t:'face', dir}`.

## Maps, exits and energy

- The world is several maps joined by exits, like FireRed's towns and routes: Stonebrook (a town), the Near Woods up its north road and the South Road down its south road (the wilds, depth 1 both) so far, plus the inside of every building (kind `inside`): a house's door (`doorOf`, the middle of its front row) is an exit into a small map of its own, and the validator refuses a house whose door leads nowhere. A house is a cabin (3 by 2) or, with `style: 'napo'`, one of NAPO's concrete buildings (3 by 2 up to 9 by 5); a NAPO building's door leads into a room marked `style: 'napo'` (concrete inside), a cabin's into a cabin's room, and the validator checks both ways. A map is a zone: the server tells you only what happens on your map (`join`, `leave`, `step`, `face`), which also keeps the traffic down as the world grows.
- An exit is a strip of tiles on a map's edge. When your step lands on one, the server confirms the step, then moves you to the arrival tile on the other map at once: the old map gets `leave`, the new one `join`, and you get `{t:'zone', map, x, y, dir, players, reason:'exit'}`. The client stops predicting on an exit tile, fades to black, swaps the map and fades in with its name.
- Energy (`energy.ts`) is the server's: every tick it moves each player's energy by `energyRate` for their tile: a refill within `FIRE_RADIUS` of a fireplace (shelters and some houses keep one burning, always), nothing in towns and insides, and a drain in the wilds that grows with the map's depth, the walking distance to the home exit (`TileMap.homeSteps`) and bad weather. Street lights (`LAMP_RADIUS`) are for seeing, not energy. You get `{t:'energy', energy:{value, max, rate}}` when the rate changes noticeably and every `ENERGY_SYNC_MS` while it changes; the client counts on between messages.
- At zero you collapse: the server puts you on the home town's spawn with full energy and sends `zone` with `reason:'collapse'`.

## Finds, the bag and piles

- Items are data (`content/items.json`, checked by `validateItems`): what each item is, its stack size, what using it does, and **find rules**: which map, which tiles (kinds, walking distance from the way home, nearness to objects such as wrecks or poles, or `around` a fixed tile), when (`when`, or while a `condition` is on), how many lie out there at once and how long a picked one takes to grow back. The rules and the bag's arithmetic are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/items.ts`.
- The server owns every find: it places them at start on tiles that fit their rule, and when someone picks one (`{t:'pick', x, y}`, on your own tile or the one next to you), it is gone for everyone (`findGone`) and a new one grows later on another tile that fits the same rule (`find`). Finds live in memory; a restart places them afresh.
- Your bag (8 slots, stacks) is the server's too; you hear it whole after any change (`bag`), plus `got` for the "+1 Glowcap" of what you picked up and `refused` with a reason when something did not happen. `use` (a thermos: +30 energy) works on a slot, and `discard` too, with a `count` (all of the slot when left out). A find that is a tool never goes into the bag: it is the picker's for good (Tools, under Equipment and the workbench).
- **What an action did** (`did`): every `feed`, `use`, `discard`, `craft` and `mend` that goes through ends with `{t:'did', did}`, queued after everything else the action changed: a `Did` (protocol.ts) is a discriminated union of the facts the text box needs, never the words: `fire` (the item, how many went in, the fuel `left` now, `lit` when it was out), `stone` (how many, the Old Stone as it stands after, `woke`), `used` (the `energy` it gave, as much as the bar had room for; a flare's seconds; a mark's way and how long it shows; what a strange object turned out to be, `into`, which no longer comes as a `got`), `made` (a tool made went to the player's tools, which the client tells by the item's kind), `mended` and `thrown` (how many). A refusal is `refused`, as before. The client words it (said.ts).
- **Live finds** (an item with `live: {xp, fresh, fade, into}`, one to a slot): picking one stamps its slot with `since`, the wall time in ms, which rides in `players.bag` (no migration) and nowhere else; the client hears its `age` in seconds in `bag` instead, and counts on from there with `liveXp` (the countdown ring and line). Each tick the World looks only at carriers (`Online.live`): a slot older than `liveEnds` becomes its `into` item. Stashed, each live slot is taken apart from the rest (`merge` would forget `since`) and `storeLive` puts one `into` in the stash with `liveXp` XP, paying nothing off `out`: the stash never holds a live item. A collapse pile holds the `into` item. Starting or stopping carrying one tells the map `glow`, and `PlayerView.live` tells newcomers; the client draws the columns of light (`LiveGlows`, a few built once, `fog: false`) over the carriers.
- Collapsing turns what you carried into a pile where you fell (one per player, a new collapse replaces it). Its owner gets everything back; anyone else gets a random half (`halfOf`) and the rest is lost; it fades an hour after the collapse (`DROP_LIFETIME_MS`). Bags are saved with the player (`players.bag`) and piles in their own table (`drops`), so both survive a restart.

## What wears you down, and what helps

The rules are in `packages/shared` (so client and server agree) and the World runs them; everything is per tick (`TICK_MS`) with no timers of its own.

- **The drain** (`energy.ts`, `energyRate(map, x, y, weather, conditions)`): on top of depth, distance and weather, factors for the bag's load (`bagLoad`: item weights over `CARRY_KG`), wetness, a hitchhiker, a surge whose front has passed your tile (`inSurge`; a street light shelters you), a storm over you and a flash discharging where you stand (`flashHits`), each cut by the resistances of what you wear. A warm tile refills only while its fire burns, as warm as it burns (`fireHeat`). The World recomputes every player's rates each tick and tells them (`energy` with a `body`: wet, wetRate, load, hitched) when a rate turns or moves more than 10%; the client counts energy and wetness on between reports.
- **Fires** (`apps/server/src/fires.ts`): every fireplace, with the game time it goes out. Fires in town and in the houses whose door opens onto a town are tended (never out, `left: null`), and a map can mark one `tended` (the old cabin's and the NAPO Bunker's: `npm run validate` wants the shelter nearest to the way home from each region next to the home town to keep one, so new players always have a safe fire); the rest start between half and full (`FIRE_MAX_S`, 30 minutes) at start-up, in memory only. `{t:'feed', x, y, slot, count?}` puts `count` (1 to `FEED_MAX`, 1 when left out) of an item with `fuel` into a fire next to you (diagonals too), from that slot first and then from others holding the same: one by one while the fire is not full (`fireFull`: within a second of `FIRE_MAX_S`; the last may top it up past it), so it takes as many as fit, and each counts for the fire keeper. `fireTakes(left, fuel)` says how many that is; the client asks with it. None fit: `fire_full`. The map hears `fire` with the seconds left, and the client counts down to draw the flames; the feeder hears `did`.
- **The sky** (`sky.ts`): `weatherAt(wall)` is the day (with `WEATHER=cycle`, the default, the World follows it every tick; a fixed `WEATHER` still works for trying things out), and `surgeAt(rule, wall)` a region's surge clock from its map's `surge` rule. Both follow the wall clock, so restarts never shift them. The World tells a map when its phase changes (`surge`), puts the rules' `when: 'unstable'` finds out while a region is restless or surging and takes them back when it is calm; `when: 'aurora'` finds likewise on aurora nights. The front's position is `surgeFront(rule, map.deepest, view)`, which the client computes too. `stormAt(rule, wall)` is a region's storm clock from its map's `storm` rule, the same shape (clear, `coming` for `warn` seconds, then the storm); a room hears the storm of the map its door opens onto, and `when: 'storm'` finds grow while one blows. **Conditions** (`conditionsAt(data, wall)`, data under `conditions` in `content/items.json`): the day is `dayIndex(wall)` (the one `weatherAt` uses; it starts at dawn), the week `weekIndex(wall)` (Monday 00:00 UTC, always a dawn). A day's dice are `seeded(dayIndex * 7919 + seed)` (mulberry32): one daily condition by `weight`, a second when a roll is under `second`, never of the same `group`; the week's is `weekly[weekIndex % length]`, next week's the one after. Nothing is stored. The World's `tick` notices a new day or week, tells everyone online (`conditions`, and the welcome has them), opens and closes find rules with a `condition` (the same path as `when`; a daily condition's finds are sown afresh every dawn, and the NAPO cache's `respawn` of a day means a picked one does not come back), gives each map's watchers the lairs of a condition that moves them (precomputed with `TileMap.lairs`, and awake ones outside them are sent away) or none while one puts them to sleep, and for `fireOut` douses one untended fire of the map or its shelters (`Fires.douse`), picked with `seeded(dayIndex)`. The first tick after start-up does all of it but put a fire out, so a restart mid-day cannot put one out again. The client draws a `fog` condition by capping the fog outdoors on that map (`Game.fogCap`, `view/world.ts`), and Mira's first line and the dawn banner come from the names. For play-tests, `CLOCK_SHIFT_MS` moves the world's clock (added to `epochOffset`; refused when `NODE_ENV=production`). Flashes are not on a clock: every `flashes.every` seconds the World picks someone out in the open within the rule's `steps` from home and starts one on a tile around them (`flash`, with `left` seconds: it glows, then discharges in its last `FLASH_BURST_S`).
- **Tall grass** (tile `h`, kind `tallgrass`) hides whoever stands in it from creatures, and only from them. The rule lives in one place, `hidden(map, x, y)` in `packages/shared/src/map.ts`, and everything creatures decide goes through it: `TileMap.creatureMayStand` (walkable, not an exit, out of the light, away from fires, not hidden) is where any creature may stand, step or wake, so `lairs`, the watchers' steps and the skulkers' paths never enter it; and the World notices only who is `noticeable` (`exposed`, and not hidden), so watchers never come for someone in it, skulkers neither hear nor see them, and a chase ends when the prey reaches it (`giveUp`, as when a skulker loses someone). `exposed` alone still starts flashes, and nothing else looks at tall grass: the drain, wetness, hitchhikers, surges and storms find you there as anywhere. A watcher faced from tall grass still freezes: `faces` is about who looks, not who is seen.
- **Watchers** (a map's `watchers` rule): server-side creatures that wake on tiles of their rule far from every player, step every `WATCHER_STEP_MS` toward the nearest noticeable player within reach along a short breadth-first path (never onto light, fire, exits, tall grass or near a flare), and freeze while any player within sight faces them (`faces`). Someone carrying a live find is prey from `WATCHER_HUNT_LIVE` (12) steps instead of `WATCHER_HUNT` (9). Reaching someone: `touched` (energy and one random unit of the bag lost), and away for a while. The map hears `creature` and `creatureGone`.
- **Skulkers** (a map's `skulkers` rule): wake like watchers, on fern tiles of their range, but only while their rule's time holds (`night`: night and aurora; `storm`: the region's storm phase), and sink away (`creatureGone`) when it ends. One lies still in its lair until a noticeable player is within `SKULKER_HEAR` walking (their last step ended under `SKULKER_HEAR_MS` ago) or `SKULKER_SEE` standing, and a path to them exists inside its range; then it steps every `SKULKER_STEP_MS` (slower than `STEP_MS`) along `pathStep` over tiles a watcher may stand on and within its steps from home, and its `creature` messages carry `chasing` (the player's id; clients rustle the ferns, play the cry and the chase loop). It gives up after `SKULKER_CHASE_MS`, when the prey is no longer noticeable (in light, by a fire, near a flare or in tall grass) or on its map, or when no way inside its range is left, notices nobody for `SKULKER_CALM_MS` and walks back to its lair (a full-map search, so it always finds the way). Catching someone: `touched` with `by: 'skulker'`, `SKULKER_CATCH` energy, and one random bag slot dropped as the player's pile (`dropBag` with a slot: no echo, replacing an older pile), then away like a watcher; a catch that empties the bar is a collapse.
- **Hitchhikers, flares, marks**: a hitchhiker is a flag on the player, rolled each tick in the dark, deep in (`hitch` tells them). A flare (`use`) lasts `use.flare` seconds on its tile (`flare` to the map); the client gives the nearest one a real light of its own, always in the scene so the shader never changes. Marks (`use` of an item with `use.mark`) are records like piles: saved in their own table (`marks`), faded after a day, at most `MARKS_PER_PLAYER` each.
- **The Old Stone**: the first `stone` object in the world. Shards (`charge`) fed to it (`feed`, with a `count`: it takes all of them, from every slot holding that item) add up; at `STONE_NEED` it wakes, burns one every `STONE_SHARD_S` and sleeps at none. Everyone online hears `stone` (an Outgoing to `'all'`). Saved in `world_state` under `stone`, with the wall time of its charge, so it keeps burning across a restart.
- **Strange objects** (`use.identify`) turn into one of their `reveals` (by weight), only in town or a house whose door opens onto one. **Charms** (kind `charm`) change `Mods` while in the bag, like feats.
- **Feats** (`feats.ts`): one table, `FEATS`, gives each feat its count (`stat`), the value of `Mods` it changes and which way (`less`, `more` or a `chance`), and five ranks (`RANKS`), each the count that reaches it (`need`) and its share (`by`). A rank is never stored: `rankOf(feat, count)` follows from the counts, so the counts saved before ranks existed are worth their rank the moment a player joins, and nothing announces it. The server counts in `players.stats` (jsonb, so a new count needs no migration): `rainSteps`, `nightSteps`, `heavySteps` and `farSteps` in `move` (`stepCounts`: out in the wilds, in the rain, in the dark, with a bag at least `HEAVY_LOAD` heavy as it feels, `FAR_STEPS` or more from home), `fed` in `feed` (once for every unit a fire takes, however many one `feed` puts in), `mended` in `mend` and `found` in `pickFind` (finds out in the wilds or a shelter off them, never piles). A count that reaches a rank (`count`) tells the player `feat` (id, rank and every count) once, since counts only go up, saves them at once and refreshes their rates. `modsOf(stats, charms)` makes one `Mods` of every rank reached and every charm carried: factors multiply (`wetting` in `wetRate`, `load` in `bagLoad`, `hitch` in the hitchhiker's roll, `warmth` by a fire, `wear` in `wearDown`, `farDrain` in `energyRate` on tiles `FAR_STEPS` or more from home), chances add up as separate tries (`double`: `pickFind` rolls `rng` for it, never for a live find, and the second unit goes in only if it fits; `got` then says `double`). The client keeps the counts the welcome, a `zone` or a `feat` told it; the status panel asks for them afresh when it opens (`{t:'stats'}`, answered with `stats`), since steps are counted without a message each, and shows each feat with `rankOf` and `rankText`. A `feat` banner waits, like a chapter's, until no panel, text box, fade or other banner is in the way.
- **Echoes**: the World keeps each player's last `TRAIL_STEPS` tiles in the wilds; a pile carries them (`drops.trail`, and `trail` in `DropView`).
- **The notice board** (a map object `board`): `{t:'board', x, y}` next to one gets `board` with plain lines about the weather, today's and this week's conditions (and next week's), the surges, fires that are low or out, the last hour's collapses and the Old Stone. Like `talk`, a look that comes in while the steps sent before it still wait in the queue (a slow network bunched them up) is answered once they are walked, from where they took the player.

## Home: the stash, XP and levels

- The rules are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/progress.ts`: `store` (into a stash, with the XP it earns), `takeOut`, `usedUp`, the XP curve (`levelOf`, `xpFor`) and `maxEnergy(level)`. A stash is `{ items, out }`: how many of each item lie in it, and how many were taken out and not brought back (those earn nothing when they return; using one up forgets it).
- A map object `chest` is a stash's chest (the home room has one). `{t:'chest', x, y}` next to one answers `chest` with the stash as a list; `{t:'store', x, y, slot?}` puts a slot (or everything) in and answers `bag`, `chest`, `progress` (XP, level, the XP the level started at and the next one needs, the bar) with what it `gained`, and the energy when a new level grew the bar; `{t:'take', x, y, item, count}` gives back what fits in the bag. The World checks the chest is next to the player every time.
- A player's energy bar is `maxEnergy(levelOf(xp))`: the `max` in every `energy` message. XP and the stash are saved with the player (`players.xp`, `players.stash`) and at once whenever items move between the bag and the stash.

## The story

- The rules are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/story.ts`; the chapters are data (`content/story.json`, checked by `validateStory` against the maps and items: the people and desks by `id`, maps by id, items by id). A chapter has an `id`, a `title`, a `text` (what the journal keeps), one `when` (a `StoryEvent`: `store`, `reach` a map, `feed` a fire or the Old Stone, `pick` an item, `talk` to a person, `read` a desk) and `hints` by person. The first chapter has no `when`: everyone starts there.
- A player's place in the story is the id of the latest chapter they reached (`players.story`, migration `010_story.sql`; none: the first chapter). The World (`moveStory`) moves it on only when the event is what the next chapter waits for, one chapter at a time, saves the player at once and tells them `chapter`. The events come from what the World already does: storing, crossing an exit or a door, feeding a fire out in the wilds (a tended one refuses, and one in town never counts) or the Old Stone, picking a find, and `{t:'talk', x, y}`, which the client sends when it opens a person's lines or a desk's text next to it (the server finds who or what stands there). The client sends it as soon as its own last step is over, so when a slow network bunches up the steps before it and they still wait in the queue, the World hears it once they are walked, from where they took the player.
- Chapters are only ever added at the end, so an id the server's story does not have comes from a newer one (a release rolled back): that player is past every chapter it has, nothing moves them on, and their id is kept for when the newer story is back.
- The client bundles `content/story.json` like the items. The welcome carries `story` (its version, and the chapter you are in); a client with another version reloads. `storyLines` puts a person's hint in front of what they always say, or after it when talking to them reaches the next chapter; `journal` lists the chapters reached for the journal panel (`journal.ts`, `hud.ts`). A chapter reached is announced with a banner once no dialog, panel or fade is in the way.
- **One place for what people say:** `storyLines` is where a person's lines are put together, so whatever else comes to change them later (lines about what you did, or about the day) goes through it, in one order after the chapter's hint, rather than beside it. The `talk` message is the one a client sends for people and desks; anything the server has to answer about them extends it. **One book:** the journal panel is the only one: later field notes and notes found out there are parts of it.

## Equipment and the workbench

- The rules are in `packages/shared/src/gear.ts`: the six slots, the five elements, `resistOf` (what the pieces worn resist, each at most `RESIST_MAX`), `bagSlotsOf`, `gearEnergy` and `canMake`. Gear is an item of kind `gear` in `content/items.json`, with its `slot`, `tier`, `resist`, `bag` (a bag's slots), `bonus` (energy) and `color` (on your character); `recipes` say what the workbench makes from what.
- A player's gear is `players.gear` (`{ slot: item }`), null for one who never chose: they wear `STARTER_GEAR`, filled in when they join. The World keeps each online player's bar (level plus gear) and bag size, and feeds `resistOf` into `energyRate` (cold, electricity, radiation) and `wetRate` (wind).
- `{t:'equip', x, y, item}` and `{t:'unequip', x, y, slot}` work next to a chest: the piece moves between the stash and the slot, without XP and without touching what was taken out; everyone on the map hears `gear` with what the player wears now (and `PlayerView` carries it, so newcomers see it). `{t:'bench', x, y}` next to a map object `workbench` (the home room has one, beside its chest) answers `bench` with the stash; `{t:'craft', x, y, recipe}` pays from the stash, puts the result in it, and answers `bench` and `did` (`made`). A recipe whose result is a tool gives the tool instead (below), and is refused with `have_tool` before anything is paid when the player owns it already; the client never asks for one you have (its workbench card says "You have it", its button greyed).
- **Tools** (items of kind `tool`: the paper maps, and later the radio, bolt cutters, waders and the maps found farther out) are each player's own for good, in the order they got them: `players.tools` (migration `012_tools.sql`), null for a player who never got one of their own, who carries `STARTER_TOOLS` (`toolsOf`). One path gives a tool however it comes, `World.giveTool(player, item)`: the workbench (a recipe that makes one), a find (a find rule whose item is a tool: picked up, it goes to the player's tools, is gone for everyone like any find and grows back by its rule, counts for the forager and the story like any pick, and never comes up double; one the player owns already is refused with `have_tool` and stays for someone else), and later a parcel or a chapter. The first tool of a player's own writes the starter tools down with it. It saves the player at once and sends `tools` (the whole list); how the tool came is for the caller to say: a find floats with `got` (`from: 'tool'`, the "+ Radio" over their head), and the workbench says it in the text box with `did` (`made`), which the client words from the item's kind ("You make a radio. It is yours for good: its button is in your bag."). A tool never goes into the bag, a pile, the stash or a trade: it has no slot and no weight, and nothing takes it away (a save without the list keeps the saved one; an id the server does not know as a tool, a newer release's, stays saved and is left out of what the player hears). `npm run validate` wants every tool to have a `name`, a `text` and an `icon` from `TOOL_ICONS` (the drawing on its button, which the client has, `icons.ts`), every starter tool to exist and be a tool, a recipe that makes a tool to make one, and nothing to be paid with, mended with or turn into a tool.
- **Pieces** (`gear.ts`: `Piece`, `pieceFactor`, `wearSeconds`, `mendCost`, `newPiece`): what a player wears is `players.worn` (`{ slot: { cond, quirk } }`, migration `009_worn.sql`); the stash keeps counting gear in `stash.items` as before, with its pieces in `stash.pieces`, so an older release still reads it, and `fitPieces` makes the pieces match the counts whenever a stash changes or a player joins (counts from before pieces become new pieces). The World wears worn pieces down in `advance` while the player is in the wilds (`wear` by tier in `content/items.json`, slower by the mender's rank: `Mods.wear`), feeds the condition into `resistOf` and `gearEnergy`, and repeats it in the body of every `energy` message (`worn`). `{t:'equip', ..., n}` picks the `n`th piece of an item in the stash; `{t:'mend', x, y, slot}` at a workbench pays `mend` (by tier) from the stash, counts once toward the mender, and answers `bench` and `did` (`mended`); gear cannot be `take`n out of the chest (`gear_stays`). Quirks (`QUIRKS`, named in `quirks` in `content/items.json`) are in `PlayerView.quirks` and the `gear` message; the client draws them (footprints and flickering lamps, `wilds.ts` and `world.ts`) and hums (`main.ts`) from the surge clock.
- The client draws gear by slot and color: an icon per slot tinted with the piece's color (`icons.ts`, `iconFor`), and the character's parts in those colors (`characters.ts`, `Look`), the bag bigger the more it holds.

## Friends and private messages

- Friends, requests, blocks, messages and reports involve players who are offline, so they are not in the World: `apps/server/src/social.ts` reads and writes storage for every action, and `net.ts` hands it each message (one player's actions run one after another) and says who is online where.
- Storage: `links` (`friend`, stored both ways; `request`, from who asked; `block`, from who blocks), `tells` (private messages, deleted when read), `reports`, and `players.requests_off`.
- Client messages: `befriend` (by `id` or `name`; if they asked you first, you are friends at once), `answer`, `unfriend` (also takes back a request), `tell`, `read`, `block` (ends a friendship and any request either way), `report`, `requests` (the setting) and `friends` (the list again). The server answers with `friends`: the whole list, with each online friend's map. After the welcome come `friends` and `tells` (every unread message); each new message to someone online is a `tells` of one.
- Refusals (`refused`, whose `action` is the message's `t`): `unknown_player`, `requests_off` (also when they blocked you, so nobody learns who blocked them), `you_blocked`, `not_friends`, `too_many` (20 requests waiting, or 50 unread from you), `slow_down` (20 messages a minute), `guest` (they play as a guest: nobody can ask them yet). With sign-in, a guest is refused every one of these messages with `sign_in_first`, checked in `Social.handle` with what the session was when the message came in (a report needs a reporter who answers for it); blocking and reporting a guest works. Nothing among friends is sent to a guest (no list or messages after the welcome, nor when someone blocks them): a guest's `joined` only reads whom they block, so chat keeps them unheard.
- The client keeps the conversation for the session only (`friends.ts`, `Game.talks`); opening someone's card sends `read`. A report carries the last thing they wrote you as the reporter's `quote`.
- **Chat** (`apps/server/src/chat.ts`, rules in `packages/shared/src/chat.ts`): `{t:'say', to: 'world' | 'local', text}` from a signed-in player (`authSub`; anyone else, a guest among them, is refused with `sign_in_first`), at most 5 in any 10 s, goes out as `said` to everyone online, or to everyone on the speaker's map within `LOCAL_REACH` (12 tiles), the speaker included, but never to someone who blocks the speaker (`Social.blocks`, kept in memory for players online). Words in `content/words.json` (next to the items; `npm run validate` checks it) are masked by `maskWords`. Nothing said is stored or logged. The client keeps the last `CHAT_LOG` lines and draws local lines as bubbles over heads.

## Connecting and signing in

The server runs in one of three sign-in modes (`AUTH_MODE`, `apps/server/src/auth.ts`), and `GET /auth-config` tells the client which. It is never cached, so the page loaded after a release that changes the mode sees it.

| Mode | `/auth-config` | Who you are |
|---|---|---|
| `legacy` (the default) | `{mode:'legacy'}` | No sign-in: a name makes a character, and its token (kept in the browser's localStorage; only its SHA-256 is stored) logs back in. |
| `dev` | `{mode:'dev', providers}` | An email, believed without a code, so each tab can be someone else. For development and tests (`npm run dev` and the local `compose.yaml` set it); refused when `NODE_ENV=production` unless `ALLOW_DEV_AUTH=1`. The identity is `dev:` and the email in lower case. `providers` only shows the Google and Apple buttons, which say they need a Supabase project. |
| `supabase` | `{mode:'supabase', url, publishableKey, providers}` | Supabase Auth: the client (`supabase.ts`, supabase-js) has a 6-digit code emailed and checks it, or signs in with one of the `providers` (Google, Apple; below), and Supabase keeps the session in the browser and refreshes it. The server checks the access token (a JWT) with jose against the project's public keys (`<url>/auth/v1/.well-known/jwks.json`, kept 10 minutes and fetched again early for a key it has not seen; HS256 with `SUPABASE_JWT_SECRET` only for projects that still sign with a shared secret): issuer `<url>/auth/v1`, audience `authenticated`, not expired (30 s of clock difference allowed), not an anonymous user. The identity is the token's `sub`, Supabase's user id, however the player signed in. |

`providers` is `AUTH_PROVIDERS` (`google`, `apple`, in the order the card shows them; empty until the Supabase project has them set up). It comes over HTTP, and zod leaves out fields a client does not know, so a page from before it reads the answer as it always did, and a name this client does not know is left out: no new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`.

**Google and Apple.** The email card shows a button for each provider listed (`providers.ts`, inline SVG logos), above the email. A tap writes down in localStorage which provider it went to, when, and for which project (`napoland.signingInWith`), then `signInWithOAuth` leaves for the provider through Supabase with the PKCE flow (`flowType: 'pkce'`: supabase-js keeps a one-time verifier in localStorage, which alone turns the code that comes back into a session; the email code is checked directly either way). The client uses that flow only when the server lists a provider, so until the owner turns one on the email code keeps the flow production has always used. The provider sends the player back to the game's address (`location.origin`, which the project's Redirect URLs allow): a new page, where supabase-js exchanges the code for the session as it starts and the client takes what is left of the answer out of the address (an error, or a code it could not use). `start()` reads the note and forgets it. With a session, the page plays as after a code: the hello carries the access token and the guest's token, and the claim, `has_character` or `need_name` follow unchanged. Without one (cancelled, refused, a code that could not be used), the email card comes back with "Signing in with Google did not finish. Try again, or use your email." under the buttons, and its way back to the guest. A note older than 10 minutes, or from another project, is forgotten without a word. A page the browser brings back from its history as it left it (`pageshow`, `persisted`) goes the same way.

**Play first, sign in to keep it.** With sign-in (dev and supabase), whoever has not signed in plays as a **guest**: a character with no `auth_sub`, made and played by its token exactly like a character without sign-in (the token in the browser, only its SHA-256 on the server), in dev mode kept by the tab (sessionStorage) so each tab can be someone else. The welcome says `guest: true`; others see `guest` in the guest's `PlayerView`. Chat and everything among friends are refused to a guest on the server (`refused` with `sign_in_first`, `action` the message's `t`), and nobody can ask a guest to be friends (`refused` `guest`); a guest can be blocked and reported, and still hears chat. A guest gets no `friends` or `tells`. `GUEST_DAYS` (30) after they last played, a guest is deleted (Saving).

1. The client asks `/auth-config`, shows the matching card (`signin.ts`, a small state machine tested without a page: play, email (with Google and Apple above it), code, name, account), then opens `/ws` and sends `hello` with `v` (protocol version, now 18) and:
   - legacy: a saved `token`, or a `name` for a new character;
   - dev and supabase, signed in: `auth` (the email, or Supabase's access token, asked for afresh before every connection), the guest's `token` kept in this browser (or one from before sign-in) if there is one, unless it was set aside for the account's own character, and a `name` once the server has asked for one;
   - dev and supabase, not signed in: the guest's `token`, or on a first visit a `name` from the play card, the first card (**Play now**, with "I have played before: sign in" under it).
2. With sign-in, the server gives the identity its character (`players.auth_sub`: one per identity). If it has one and the hello brings the token of a guest (a character nobody has claimed), the server answers `has_character` with the character's `name` instead of playing it: the client's account card asks "Play as <name>? Your guest character stays in this browser.", and says hello again without the token (it keeps it aside, `napoland.guestSetAside`, until you sign out), or signs out of the account in this browser and plays the guest on. Without a character of its own, the character of the hello's `token`, if nobody has claimed it yet, becomes theirs (`claimed: true`): that is how a guest, or a character made before sign-in, carries over, by signing in on the browser that made it. Without that, a new character with the `name` (the name rules and the limit of new players per address apply); without a name, `need_name`, and the client asks "Choose a name for your character". An `auth` that proves nothing (malformed, expired, forged, for another project) gets `sign_in_required`: the client refreshes its session once and reconnects, and after that asks to sign in again. If Supabase's keys cannot be fetched, the connection closes with 1011 and the client tries again later.
3. Without `auth` on a server with sign-in, the hello is a guest's. Its `token` plays its character, unless someone has signed in with it since: then `sign_in_required` (only they play it, signed in; the client forgets the token and shows the play card, saying the character is kept with the account), and an unknown token gets `unknown_token` (the play card). The server marks the guest seen (`players.last_seen_at`) before it plays, so the cleanup never takes a guest who is back. A `name` makes a new guest, held to the name rules and the limit of new players per address like anyone new. A hello with neither gets `sign_in_required`.
4. The server answers `welcome`: your id and name, the map you are on (id and version; a client with another version reloads) with everyone, every find and every pile on it, `stepMs`, the weather, your energy and body, your bag, the fires, marks, creatures, flares, flashes, and surge and storm clocks of your map, the Old Stone, your feat counts, your tools (the items of kind `tool` you own, in the order you got them, kept apart from the bag, `toolsOf`: until you get one of your own they are `STARTER_TOOLS`, a map of every area there is: Stonebrook, the Near Woods and the South Road; see Equipment and the workbench; the bag's header shows every map you own as the one map button and each other tool as a button of its own, in the order you got them (`toolViews` in the client's `items.ts`; a tapped tool says what it is in the text box), and the map button, or M, opens the map whose `chart` is the area you are in (`mapFor` in `papermap.ts`: a room's area is the map its door opens onto) or says you have no map of this place; it draws a tool's `chart` once per map version in `papermap.ts`, never with anyone on it: the ground, water, roads, tall grass (a hatch of short strokes, and a key in the margin on a map that has any), cabins and NAPO's flat-roofed buildings, poles and wires, masts, fences, cars and signs, and in handwriting the doors' names, the way home and the map's `places`, each name moved a line up or down until it covers no other), the version of `content/items.json` and where you are in the story (the version of `content/story.json` and your chapter; another version of either also makes the client reload). Without sign-in (legacy) and for a guest it has your `token`; `guest` says whether you play as a guest, and `claimed: true` that this sign-in just made a guest, or a character from before sign-in, yours (the client says so: "Signed in. Your character is yours to keep."). Everyone on your map gets `join`.
5. Errors are explicit: `bad_version` makes the client reload, `unknown_token` shows the name card (legacy) or the play card (a guest's token), `bad_name` explains the problem, `need_name`, `sign_in_required` and `has_character` as above, `replaced` means the same player connected from another screen, `server_full` means try again soon.
6. Phones drop connections often; the client reconnects with backoff and says hello again (with a refreshed access token when it signs in).

The hello may be up to 9 KiB, since a Supabase token grows with the profile a provider such as Google adds; every other message stays under 1 KiB. The client prepares its hello before it opens the socket, so a token refresh never eats into the 5 s the server waits for it. Tokens and emails are never logged.

## Limits

No single client can wear the server down (`net.ts`, `limits.ts`):

- Each connection: messages of at most 1 KiB (the hello 9 KiB), 30 a second (bursts of 60), `hello` within 5 s, an answer to the heartbeat ping every 30 s. At most `MAX_PLAYERS` (500) online.
- Each address: `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_IP` (20) open connections; one more gets HTTP 429 before a WebSocket exists. `NEW_PLAYERS_PER_IP_PER_HOUR` (10) new players in any hour, new guests among them, counted in memory; one more gets `bad_name` ("Too many new players from your network"). Coming back to a character (with a token, with sign-in, or claiming one) is never limited.
- The address is the socket's. Behind our own proxy (Caddy), `TRUST_PROXY=1` makes it the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, the one the proxy added; the game port must then only be reachable through the proxy, or clients could claim any address. Addresses are never logged.

## Saving

Friends, requests, blocks, unread messages and reports are written at once (`social.ts`). Players (position, map, energy, bag, wetness, feat counts, XP, stash, gear, the chapter of the story, their tools) live in memory while online and are written to PostgreSQL every 15 s, when they leave and on shutdown, and at once whenever items move between a bag and a pile (or a watcher takes one, a skulker makes one fall, a feat's rank is reached, a chapter reached, or a tool given). A player's tools (`players.tools`, migration `012_tools.sql`) stay null until they get one of their own, which reads as the starter tools; a save that carries none keeps what was saved (`COALESCE`, like the chapter), so nothing takes a tool away, and the previous release, which never writes the column, leaves it alone. A player's row also says whose it is (`auth_sub`, empty for a guest or a character made before sign-in until someone claims it), holds the hash of the token of a character made without sign-in, and says when they last played (`last_seen_at`, set by every save and when a guest comes back). With sign-in, a guest (no `auth_sub`) not seen for `GUEST_DAYS` (30) is deleted at start-up, before the piles and marks are loaded, and once a day after (`forgetGuests`), but only once the server has applied the rule for `GUEST_DAYS` (`guests_since` in `world_state`, stored the first time: the privacy policy promises to say such a change in the game before it takes effect, and characters from before sign-in count as guests). Their pile, marks, links and unread messages go with the row, a report about them stays without them; a partial index on `last_seen_at` for rows without `auth_sub`, migration `011_guests.sql`, keeps it cheap. A character someone signed in with is never deleted, and without sign-in (legacy) nothing is. Piles and marks are written on every change and loaded at start (the last hour's piles, the last day's marks; older ones are deleted), and so is the Old Stone (`world_state`); finds, fires, creatures and flares live in memory only. Schema changes are SQL files in `apps/server/migrations`, applied in order at start-up and recorded in `schema_migrations`; they only add, so the previous release still runs if a new one rolls back.

## Rendering

three.js with low-poly geometry, flat faceted normals, a three-step toon ramp and back-face outlines. The terrain is one mesh built from the tile map (flat tops, vertical walls for ledges and banks), colored at every tile corner by `Ground` (`view/grass.ts`): low-frequency value noise seeded by the map's id, dry and damp patches, darker and more olive where the forest or a lone fir shades the ground, damp by water, a slight jitter per tile, and their own variation for asphalt (darker mended patches), lots and mud; the mesh blends the corners, so there is no checkerboard and a place looks the same on every visit (floors and walls indoors keep their own look). Trees (a tree object, or one per forest tile, varied by position), ferns and grass are instanced in blocks of 8x8 tiles with computed bounds so blocks off screen are skipped (the Near Woods has thousands of trees); rocks, mushrooms and road lines are instanced too. Grass is clumps of three-sided blades placed by tile position (`grassClumps`): one to three low tufts on a grass tile, about fourteen knee-high clumps on a tile of tall grass, one instanced mesh a block for each, their normals bent toward the sky so they are lit like the ground they grow from. One toon material sways them in the vertex shader (`GrassMaterial`, an `onBeforeCompile`): a gust across the map, harder in a storm, bending a blade more the higher it goes, and a parting around the four players nearest you. Time, wind and those four are uniforms that change every frame without a new shader, every map's grass shares one program (`customProgramCacheKey`), and nothing about it is worked out per blade on the CPU. Whoever stands on tall grass crouches (`crouchToward`: lower, leaning forward, arms in, the flashlight with them); every client sees it, since it knows every player's tile. The props that never move (houses, cars, signs, lamps, poles, barrels, fences; NAPO's buildings, signs and masts from `view/napo.ts`) are hundreds of little outlined boxes, so each map joins them into a few meshes (`bake` in `view/toon.ts`): one for every plain color (as vertex colors), one for all their outlines, and one for each material that glows (lit windows, lamp heads, car lights, NAPO's screens, the masts' red lights, which blink). The whole town's props then cost about six draw calls instead of a hundred and more; they are a few thousand triangles, cheaper drawn whole than split. What moves (people, the Old Stone's crystal and rocks) stays separate. Street lamps and fires share four real lights, nearest first, handed over as you walk; the rest glow through their material, and the scene always holds the same lights (inside or out), so switching maps never recompiles shaders. Insides (`view/interior.ts`, `fire.ts`) are a plank floor, log walls (in NAPO's rooms, concrete slabs and courses of block) with the front row cut down to a baseboard so this camera sees in, furniture and a hearth whose fire burns the same in any weather; around a room everything is black, and rain, mist and wisps stay outside. A room without a fireplace is cold and dark. Outside, a house's doorway glows when its room has a fire, and its chimney smokes (`view/lighting.ts` decides how each kind of map looks in each weather). Finds and piles (`view/loot.ts`) are small glowing models, one instanced mesh per kind of item rebuilt only when the lists change, with a soft pool of light under each so they read as colored lights in the dark; your own pile has a ring in your jacket color. The world is drawn at up to twice the screen's CSS pixels; when frames stay slow (fewer than about 42 a second for 1.5 s of play) it is drawn a step coarser (`quality.ts`, down to half, never below one pixel a CSS pixel), and a step finer again once they come easily for 6 s; a step up that does not hold is tried again only after a minute, then two, then four, so the picture never flickers between two sharpnesses. One WebGL renderer lives for the whole session; each map gets a view that frees everything it made when you leave. No shadow maps: round blob shadows under things, cheaper on phones and closer to the FireRed look. Weather (overcast, rain, night) changes lights, fog, rain and glow; at night your character carries a flashlight.

The camera keeps the same circle of world (radius 6.2 tiles) around you on every screen shape: it fits the short side, and a longer screen only adds view at the edges. On upright screens the view shifts so your character is above your thumbs.

## Interface

HTML over the canvas, sized with container query units from the screen's short side, so it scales from small phones to desktops and works in any orientation: joystick bottom left (four directions with a dead zone), A and B bottom right, status top left (your name, the players here, the connection and a slim energy bar: red below 25%, pulsing below 10%, a bright tip while refilling; for a guest, a Guest label and a Sign in button under the name, a target of its own; a tap anywhere else on the panel opens the Status tab), one menu button top right (no minimap and no marker: the paper maps open from the bag's header and never show where you are), the text box and the bag where they never cover A and B. The bag shows its slots (eight in the backpack everyone starts with) with an icon per item (`icons.ts`, inline SVG) and a count; a slot opens its details with Use and Throw away (both ask first, below), and B backs out one step at a time. A picks up what lies on your tile or the one you face before it talks; tapping a find walks there and picks it up. Below 20% energy the screen's edges darken. Arriving on a map (logging in, an exit, a collapse) fades in from black under a banner with the map's name, or with what happened. The menu's About (`about.ts`) opens where the bag does: who makes and publishes the game, the source code first (the AGPL offers it to everyone who plays), the privacy policy, the legal notice, the licenses and the version the server runs; every sign-in card ends with the same links in small print. The fonts (Fredoka and Nunito, from `@fontsource-variable`) are bundled with the client, so the page loads nothing from other sites.

In the chest and at the workbench a tap looks and a second step acts (`details.ts`, plain logic tested without a page): a tap opens the card of what it is on (a `DetailRef`: a slot of the bag, something in the stash, what a slot wears, a recipe, a mend), which `detailView` builds from the game (`HudHandlers.details`), and the Hud draws it (`cardHtml`) and keeps it current as the game changes, closing it when what it is about is gone. Its one button, A, or a second tap on the same thing within `DOUBLE_TAP_MS` (`DoubleTap`) does what it says (store, take, equip, unequip, craft, mend) and closes it; the second tap may land on the card itself, which can open under the finger, and counts when it is within `DOUBLE_TAP_SLOP` of the first, so the list never moves before a double tap could come (a hidden piece is scrolled into sight after it). B closes the card before its panel. In portrait the card docks under the list, which scrolls above it; in landscape the panel grows toward A and B, never over them, and shows it beside the list. Making and mending leave the panel through one function (`Hud.bench`, which calls `craft`), where asking first wraps them (below): Make or Mend greyed out (the stash is short) shakes and keeps its card open, and still goes to the game, which says in the text box what the stash lacks and sends nothing. A piece's name comes from `pieceName`, which upgrades will give a level.

**Asking first, and saying what it did.** One text box holds someone's lines, a question, or what it says by itself. The question is `ask.ts`, plain state tested without a page: `Question` (YES chosen to start, up and down choose, left and right count between `min` and `max`, a question whose words say how many is a function of the count) and `Repeat` (holding − or + or a side of the stick: a step at once, then after a moment faster and faster). Anything that uses something up asks through one call, `Game.ask({ who, text, count?: { min, max, start? }, yes(n), no? })`: feeding a fire (`tend`: the best fuel by the bag's slot rule, how many up to `fireTakes` and `FEED_MAX`) or the Old Stone (`offer`), `use` and `discard` from the bag, `craft` and `mend` at the workbench (the one place both are sent from: a card's button, A on the card and a double tap on a row all reach them through `Hud.bench`); later features ask the same way. While it asks, the Game routes the stick, A, B and taps to it and nobody walks (`driveMe`); main.ts sends A and B to it before a card or any open panel, and the stick does not close the panel it was asked from. When the Game already knows it cannot happen it says why instead (`inform`). YES sends the message (to wherever that item lies now, `actOn`) and keeps the question up, `waiting`, until `did` or `refused` takes its place. What the box says by itself (`Game.note`) closes at its time (`noteMs`: about 4 seconds, longer for more to read), with any press (which then does nothing else) or when you walk; a note that comes while a question or someone's lines have the box waits for them (`later`). The words are `said.ts`, plain functions tested against the real items: the questions, why not, and `didText` from a `Did`, with each item's `noun` and `plural` from content/items.json ("3 resin", "a shard", "rubber gloves") and the `about` line of whatever a strange object turned into. hud.ts draws it (`setAsk`, `setNote`, told only when `Game.boxChanges` moves; `setDialog` every frame, writing only what changed): YES over NO in a frame at the right with a caret on the chosen one, − ×N + under the question, buttons at least a fingertip (`--hit`); for a note, a thin line along the bottom running out (one Web Animation per note). While it asks or says something, the box stands above the panels (the bag, the workbench) over a transparent scrim that takes a tap anywhere else (NO, or closing it), with the stick and A and B above the scrim; in landscape the stick hides as it does while someone talks, so touch answers in the box.

On a computer the keyboard drives the same handlers as the stick and the buttons (`keys.ts`, plain logic tested without a page): WASD and the arrow keys by `KeyboardEvent.code`, E or Space for A, Q or Escape for B, Enter for the chat (in the chat's line Enter sends, and on an empty line, like Escape, closes it: `chatKey` in `hud.ts`), M for the map of where you are (by the letter it types, `KeyboardEvent.key`, since layouts move it: on a French one the key in its place types a comma; a layout without Latin letters goes by where it sits: `mapKey`). The keys held form a stack, the newest wins, and they are all let go when the window loses focus or the tab is hidden, so nobody keeps walking. The game leaves alone keys with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd, keys typed into fields, Enter and Space on a button reached with the keyboard (it presses itself; a button pressed by a tap or a click lets go of the focus at once, so Enter still opens the chat) and every key while the sign-in cards are up.

## Content

The world is data in `content/maps/*.json`: what the map is (town or wilds, depth), rows of tile letters (`t` is dense forest, `h` tall grass; `TILE_CHARS` in `map.ts` has them all), rows of height levels, exits to other maps, and a list of objects (trees, cabins, lamps, signs with their text, people with their lines and their colors, `look`; NAPO's buildings, warning signs, masts, `antenna`, and desks, `console`, which you read like a sign under their `name`; a fire in the open can have a `name`, which the notice board says). A map can also list its `places`, the names people call spots by (the ring of stones, the sinks), which its paper map writes in. The maps are generated from fixed seeds (`npm run gen:map` for Stonebrook, `npm run gen:woods` for the Near Woods, `npm run gen:south` for the South Road), so the world never reshuffles and players can share routes; `gen:woods` sows its tall grass last, from hand-picked tiles and only onto grass nothing else uses (off the ways, out of the lights, clear of doors, signs, poles and named places), so adding it moved nothing else; the rooms inside buildings are drawn by hand in `tools/gen-interiors.ts` (`npm run gen:interiors`, run after the others: it checks that every door leads into its room and back out in front of it). `npm run gen` runs all four in that order. `npm run validate` checks every map on its own (sizes, tile letters, overlapping objects, a walkable spawn, reachable tiles, exits on walkable tiles, a way home in the wilds, signs and people you can stand in front of, tall grass you can walk into that is never an exit or in front of a door, sign or person, and a warning where it could hide nobody) and then how they fit together (every exit leads to a real map, onto walkable ground that is not another exit, and every map can be reached from town).

## Running it

- **Development:** `npm run dev` (server in memory on :8080, Vite on :5173 with hot reload, reachable from phones on the same Wi-Fi). Sign-in is dev mode: any email, no code.
- **Docker:** `docker compose up -d --build` builds one small image (Node + bundled server + built client + content + migrations) next to PostgreSQL 17. Settings in `.env`. Sign-in is dev mode here too (`AUTH_MODE` in `.env` changes it).
- **Sign-in:** `AUTH_MODE` is `legacy` (the default), `dev`, or `supabase` with `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` (both public; `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` is the older name). `AUTH_PROVIDERS` (`google,apple`, or one of them) lists the providers the sign-in card offers besides the email code, once the Supabase project has them set up; a name it does not know stops the server, and in dev mode it only shows their buttons (`AUTH_PROVIDERS=google,apple npm run dev` to see them). Production runs `supabase`; what the Supabase project needs is in [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md).
- **Checking a server:** `/health` answers `ok`, the players online, the uptime and `version` (from `APP_VERSION`, default `dev`), so a deploy can be checked from outside. The About panel shows that `version` too, linked to exactly that commit's code when it is one.
- **Maps:** the server loads every map in `MAPS_DIR` (default `content/maps`) and refuses to start if any fails validation; `HOME_MAP` (default `stonebrook`) is where new players start and collapsed players wake up; `ITEMS_FILE` (default `content/items.json`) holds the items and find rules, checked against the maps at start; `STORY_FILE` (default `content/story.json`) holds the story's chapters, checked against the maps and items.

## Growing later

The design's layers (your cabin, your street, the town square, the wilds) map onto the same server code: a zone is a map plus the players in it, and players already only hear about their own map. When one process is not enough, zones move to separate processes (cabins and streets start on demand, the town square and busy regions get copies), with a small message bus between them for chat, parties and cross-zone presence; `zone` messages already tell the client everything it needs when it changes maps.

Production (one AWS server with Caddy, CI on every push, automatic releases with rollback, nightly backups) is described in [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md). Still to come: a scheduled bot that signs in with a saved token, plays a little and reports when something breaks.
