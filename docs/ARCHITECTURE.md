# napoland: architecture

How the game is built, and why it is built that way. The main constraint: Claude builds, tests, play-tests, releases and maintains every part, so everything is text in one repository, one language (TypeScript) runs everywhere, and every check is a command.

## Pieces

```
 phone / desktop browser                       one server (Docker)
 +---------------------------+   WebSocket    +-------------------------------+     +------------+
 | apps/client               |  /ws (JSON)    | apps/server                   |     | PostgreSQL |
 |  three.js world  (view/)  | <------------> |  net.ts    sessions, limits   | --> | players    |
 |  HUD + controls  (hud.ts) |                |  world.ts  rules, no I/O      |     +------------+
 |  prediction      (game.ts)|   HTTP         |  http.ts   /health, client,   |
 |  sign-in      (signin.ts) | <------------> |            /auth-config       |
 +---------------------------+                |  auth.ts   who is signing in  |
               |                              |  storage.ts memory | postgres |
               | email code, Google, Apple    +-------------------------------+
               v                                              |
 +---------------------------+   its public keys (JWKS)       |
 | Supabase Auth             | <------------------------------+
 +---------------------------+
            both import packages/shared: maps, movement and energy rules, protocol
```

- **packages/shared** is the contract: `TileMap` (walkable tiles, exits, lamp light and the distance home, from the map data), movement (`STEP_MS`, `findPath`), energy (`energyRate`), the protocol (zod schemas for every client message, types for every server message, how to sign in) and content validation. Client and server can never disagree about the rules because they run the same code.
- **apps/server** owns the world. `world.ts` holds the rules as plain functions of state and time (easy to test, replayable); `net.ts` turns WebSocket messages into world calls and world events into messages; `auth.ts` checks who is signing in; `storage.ts` keeps players in PostgreSQL (or in memory for tests and quick development).
- **apps/client** draws the world and sends intentions. It predicts your own steps so walking feels instant, and animates everyone else from the server's reports.
- **Supabase Auth** only proves who someone is (an email and a code, or a Google or Apple account). The game keeps its own players and everything they own in its own database.

## Movement: authoritative server, predicted client

- The world is a grid. A step moves one tile in 200 ms (`STEP_MS`), one speed for everyone.
- The client sends `{t:'step', dir, seq}`. The server accepts it when the player's previous step has finished (40 ms tolerance for network jitter) and the target tile is walkable; early steps wait in a queue of two. On average nobody can walk faster than one tile per 200 ms, however fast they send.
- Accepted: everyone gets `{t:'step', id, x, y, dir}`; the mover also gets its `seq` back. Refused: the mover gets `{t:'reject', seq, x, y, dir}` with the real position and snaps back.
- The client walks immediately (prediction) and stays at most two steps ahead of the server's confirmations. Other players are drawn sliding from where they are to the reported tile over one step's time.
- Turning in place sends `{t:'face', dir}`.

## Maps, exits and energy

- The world is several maps joined by exits, like FireRed's towns and routes: Stonebrook (a town), the Near Woods up its north road and the South Road down its south road (the wilds, depth 1 both) so far, plus the inside of every building (kind `inside`): a house's door (`doorOf`, the middle of its front row) is an exit into a small map of its own, and the validator refuses a house whose door leads nowhere (or anywhere but an inside, but for the one house in the home town that is the way onto the street: Your street, below). A house is a cabin (3 by 2; with `curtains`, one whose people left, never lit), with `style: 'napo'` one of NAPO's concrete buildings (3 by 2 up to 9 by 5), or with `style: 'mill'` the old sawmill (5 to 9 by 2 to 4); a NAPO building's door leads into a room marked `style: 'napo'` (concrete inside), the mill's onto a floor marked `style: 'mill'` (boards inside), a cabin's into a cabin's room, and the validator checks both ways. The server tells you only what happens in your zone, your copy of your map (Zones, below: `join`, `leave`, `step`, `face`), which also keeps the traffic down as the world grows.
- An exit is a strip of tiles on a map's edge. When your step lands on one, the server confirms the step, then moves you to the arrival tile on the other map at once, into the copy of it `copyFor` decides (Zones): the old zone gets `leave`, the new one `join`, and you get `{t:'zone', map, x, y, dir, players, reason:'exit'}`. The client stops predicting on an exit tile, fades to black, swaps the map and fades in with its name.
- Energy (`energy.ts`) is the server's: every tick it moves each player's energy by `energyRate` for their tile: a refill within `FIRE_RADIUS` of a fireplace (shelters and some houses keep one burning, always), nothing in towns and insides, and a drain in the wilds that grows with the map's depth, the walking distance to the home exit (`TileMap.homeSteps`) and bad weather. Street lights (`LAMP_RADIUS`) are for seeing, not energy. You get `{t:'energy', energy:{value, max, rate}}` when the rate changes noticeably and every `ENERGY_SYNC_MS` while it changes; the client counts on between messages.
- At zero you collapse: the server wakes you up at home with full energy (`World.wakeUp`: the `wake` point of the home, in your own copy of it; Home, below) and sends `zone` with `reason:'collapse'`.

## Zones

- A **zone** is a map plus a copy key (`Zone` in `world.ts`). Each map's **main copy** (key `''`) is the world everyone shares. Any other **copy** of a map is a zone of its own with a key of its own: a player's own cabin (a private room, keyed by the player's id: Home, below), each street of neighbors (a copy of the street, keyed by the street's number: Your street, below), and the numbered copies of a crowded town square or region of the wilds, `2`, `3` and on, with the rooms off it (Crowds, below). A zone goes by `zoneKey(map, copy)`: the map's id for its main copy, `<map>:<copy>` for another, so a main copy is addressed as its map always was. Map ids are lowercase words joined by hyphens (`validateMap`), so no two zones share a key.
- **Each zone's own:** who is in it, so who hears whom (`join`, `leave`, `step`, `face`, `gear`, `glow`, `outfit`, local chat's reach and a call's); what lies there to pick up (a copy of a map with find rules grows finds of its own, by the same rules); its fires (one `Fires` a zone: a copy's burn down, or are fed, apart from the main copy's, and remember who fed them there, for thanks; a tended fireplace stays tended in every copy); its creatures (each copy has its watchers and skulkers, and they notice, chase and touch only whoever is in their zone); its flares and flashes (a flash starts near someone in the zone); its piles, marks (thanked only from their own copy) and crates' things, each of which remembers its copy (`drops.zone`, `marks.zone`, `cache_items.zone`), and the echoes the piles carry.
- **Each map's, in every copy:** the sky, the surge and storm clocks and their phases (`surge` and `storm` reach every copy of the map at the same moment, and restless or storm finds grow and go in all of them together), and the day's conditions (a condition moves or puts to sleep the watchers of every copy, and the fire it puts out overnight is out in every copy, a copy that opens later that day included). The Old Stone is the whole world's: shards fed to it from any copy go into the one stone. The notice board tells the fires of the main copies, the world everyone shares.
- **The client never learns the copy:** to it, a copy of a map is that map. The welcome, `zone` and the friends list name the map alone, and nothing about them changed (no new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`). On a street it learns its street's lots (`street`), never the street's number. The World queues a message for everyone in a zone by the zone's key (`Outgoing` `'*'`, whose `map` is that key), and the network layer keeps one audience a zone: it hears a player's zone from the World when they join (`zoneOf`) and beside the `zone` message that moves them (`Outgoing.zone`, only for a copy other than the main one; never sent).
- **One place decides the copy:** `World.copyFor(player, map)`, asked for every exit, for where a collapse wakes a player and for where one whose map is gone starts over. It gives a player's own id for a private map (`private: true`, a home: their cabin), the number of their street for the street (`lotOf`, which gives them a lot the first time they come home), and anywhere else what the crowd decides (Crowds, below).
- **Coming back:** a player's row keeps the copy they are in (`players.zone`, `''` for the main copy, migration `017_zones.sql`), saved like the map. When they join again they go back into it if it still makes sense (a private map's is always their own, and the street's always their own street; any other copy only while it is in use, someone being in it or in a room of it, and has room for them), else wherever `copyFor` sends anyone coming in (`rejoin`).
- **Open and closed:** a main copy is open as long as the World runs. Any other copy opens when someone walks into it: its wild fires start between half and full as at start-up, the finds of its map's open rules lie there, and its creatures wake on the next tick where nobody is. It closes on the tick after its last player left (`closeEmptied`), so an empty copy costs nothing: nothing per tick looks at a copy nobody is in. Its piles and marks outlive it (kept by zone key, in memory and in storage, until they fade) and lie there again when it opens again, and so do its crates when something lies in them; its finds, fires and creatures start afresh, as after a restart.
- Everything kept per zone is reached from the zone itself (a player's `Online.zone`, a find's, a creature's), and piles and marks by tile are indexed by zone key, so nothing looks through the copies of other maps, or the other copies of a map, to answer for one.
- **Crowds** ([crowded-copies](../roadmap/crowded-copies.md)): a copy of a town square holds `TOWN_CROWD` (150) players, a copy of a region of the wilds `REGION_CROWD` (300), counting whoever is in the rooms off it: a room (every inside but a home of one's own) follows the copy of the place its door opens onto (`placeOf`), entered from a copy into that copy of it and left back into that copy, so a copy is a place with its rooms. Anyone else arriving (an exit from another place, waking up where there is no home of one's own, coming back into the game where their copy is no more) comes into the copy of the place that has most of their friends online, if it has room (friends are asked both ways, `World.friends`: the World knows those of whoever is online, and a player's own only once social.ts has read them, just after they join); else the first copy with room, the main one first, then by number; else a new copy, the first number free (`crowdCopy`, `peopleIn`: a copy is in use while it, or a room of it, is open). A test sets fewer (`WorldOptions.crowd`, `ServerOptions.crowd`), and so can a play-test with a few tabs (`TOWN_CROWD` and `REGION_CROWD`, refused when `NODE_ENV=production`). No client learns a copy, as ever; the friends list says the map alone.

## Finds, the bag and piles

- Items are data (`content/items.json`, checked by `validateItems`): what each item is, its stack size, what using it does, and **find rules**: which map, which tiles (kinds, walking distance from the way home, nearness to objects such as wrecks or poles, or `around` a fixed tile), when (`when`, or while a `condition` is on), how many lie out there at once and how long a picked one takes to grow back. The rules and the bag's arithmetic are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/items.ts`.
- The server owns every find: it places them at start on tiles that fit their rule, and when someone picks one (`{t:'pick', x, y}`, on your own tile or the one next to you), it is gone for everyone (`findGone`) and a new one grows later on another tile that fits the same rule (`find`). Finds live in memory; a restart places them afresh.
- Your bag (8 slots, stacks) is the server's too; you hear it whole after any change (`bag`), plus `got` for the "+1 Glowcap" of what you picked up and `refused` with a reason when something did not happen. `use` (a thermos: +30 energy) works on a slot, and `discard` too, with a `count` (all of the slot when left out). A find that is a tool never goes into the bag: it is the picker's for good (Tools, under Equipment and the workbench).
- **What an action did** (`did`): every `feed`, `use`, `discard`, `craft`, `mend`, `upgrade`, `open`, `thank`, `cacheLeave` and `cacheTake` that goes through ends with `{t:'did', did}`, queued after everything else the action changed: a `Did` (protocol.ts) is a discriminated union of the facts the text box needs, never the words: `fire` (the item, how many went in, the fuel `left` now, `lit` when it was out), `stone` (how many, the Old Stone as it stands after, `woke`), `used` (the `energy` it gave, as much as the bar had room for; a flare's seconds; a mark's way and how long it shows; what a strange object turned out to be, `into` (a piece of gear with its `piece`, quirk rolled), which no longer comes as a `got`; `lift`, the charm and the `energy` it gave back, as far as the bar had room: a pale moth for a crushed glowcap), `made` (a tool made went to the player's tools, which the client tells by the item's kind), `mended` and `thrown` (how many, and a piece's level), `upgraded` (the level it is now, and `failed` when it did not take: its materials are spent all the same), `opened` (what a lockbox held, `got`), `thanked` (whom, by id and name, and for a fire or an arrow), and at a crate `left` and `took` (the item; whose it was, and whether taking it thanked them). A refusal is `refused`, as before. The client words it (said.ts), naming items the way the notice board does (`nounOf`, `pluralOf`, `aOf`, `amount` in packages/shared items.ts).
- **Live finds** (an item with `live: {xp, fresh, fade, into}`, one to a slot): picking one stamps its slot with `since`, the wall time in ms, which rides in `players.bag` (no migration) and nowhere else; the client hears its `age` in seconds in `bag` instead, and counts on from there with `liveXp` (the countdown ring and line). Each tick the World looks only at carriers (`Online.live`): a slot older than `liveEnds` becomes its `into` item. Stashed, each live slot is taken apart from the rest (`merge` would forget `since`) and `storeLive` puts one `into` in the stash with `liveXp` XP, paying nothing off `out`: the stash never holds a live item. A collapse pile holds the `into` item. Starting or stopping carrying one tells the map `glow`, and `PlayerView.live` tells newcomers; the client draws the columns of light (`LiveGlows`, a few built once, `fog: false`) over the carriers.
- Collapsing turns what you carried into a pile where you fell (one per player, a new collapse replaces it). Its owner gets everything back; anyone else gets a random half (`halfOf`) and the rest is lost; it fades an hour after the collapse (`DROP_LIFETIME_MS`). Bags are saved with the player (`players.bag`) and piles in their own table (`drops`), so both survive a restart.
- **Carried gear** (items.ts, gear.ts): gear stacks one to a slot, and its slot carries its `piece` (condition, quirk, level) in the bag, in `players.bag` and in a pile's `drops.items`, all jsonb, so no migration. `addAllToBag` keeps a piece on its new slot and on what is left out; a pile is made with `gather` (the same items joined like `merge`, each piece apart with its piece), and `halfOf` draws units that keep theirs, so a piece comes out of a pile as it went in, for its owner or in someone else's half. `merge` still counts for the `got` float. The World gives every unit of gear in a bag or a restored pile a piece (`World.pieced`: a saved piece if it is one, else `newPiece`), so gear saved before pieces travelled (a strange object's, which waited as a count) becomes a piece on join; what a strange object turns into (`addNew`) and gear found out there are pieces the moment they land in the bag, anomalous ones with their quirk. `bag` messages carry the pieces; `did.into` carries a revealed piece. A trade moves a piece as it is, from one bag to the other (Friends and private messages: trades).

## What wears you down, and what helps

The rules are in `packages/shared` (so client and server agree) and the World runs them; everything is per tick (`TICK_MS`) with no timers of its own.

- **The drain** (`energy.ts`, `energyRate(map, x, y, weather, conditions)`): on top of depth, distance and weather, factors for the bag's load (`bagLoad`: item weights over `CARRY_KG`), wetness, a hitchhiker, a surge whose front has passed your tile (`inSurge`; a street light shelters you), a storm over you and a flash discharging where you stand (`flashHits`), each cut by the resistances of what you wear, and last `drain`, the whole of it gentler while you are cozy (Home: A cozy cabin). A warm tile refills only while its fire burns, as warm as it burns (`fireHeat`). The World recomputes every player's rates each tick and tells them (`energy` with a `body`: wet, wetRate, load, hitched) when a rate turns or moves more than 10%; the client counts energy and wetness on between reports.
- **Fires** (`apps/server/src/fires.ts`): every fireplace, with the game time it goes out. Fires in town and in the houses whose door opens onto a town are tended (never out, `left: null`), and a map can mark one `tended` (the old cabin's and the NAPO Bunker's: `npm run validate` wants the shelter nearest to the way home from each region next to the home town to keep one, so new players always have a safe fire); the rest start between half and full (`FIRE_MAX_S`, 30 minutes) at start-up, in memory only. `{t:'feed', x, y, slot, count?}` puts `count` (1 to `FEED_MAX`, 1 when left out) of an item with `fuel` into a fire next to you (diagonals too), from that slot first and then from others holding the same: one by one while the fire is not full (`fireFull`: within a second of `FIRE_MAX_S`; the last may top it up past it), so it takes as many as fit, and each counts for the fire keeper. `fireTakes(left, fuel)` says how many that is; the client asks with it. None fit: `fire_full`. The map hears `fire` with the seconds left, and the client counts down to draw the flames; the feeder hears `did`. A fire that burns down also remembers the last `FEEDERS_KEPT` (3) players who fed it, the latest first, each once (`Fires.fedBy`, in memory like its fuel), and its `FireView` names them (`fed`), for thanks.
- **The sky** (`sky.ts`): `weatherAt(wall)` is the day (with `WEATHER=cycle`, the default, the World follows it every tick; a fixed `WEATHER` still works for trying things out), and `surgeAt(rule, wall)` a region's surge clock from its map's `surge` rule. Both follow the wall clock, so restarts never shift them. The World tells a map when its phase changes (`surge`), puts the rules' `when: 'unstable'` finds out while a region is restless or surging and takes them back when it is calm; `when: 'aurora'` finds likewise on aurora nights. The front's position is `surgeFront(rule, map.deepest, view)`, which the client computes too. `stormAt(rule, wall)` is a region's storm clock from its map's `storm` rule, the same shape (clear, `coming` for `warn` seconds, then the storm); a room hears the storm of the map its door opens onto, and `when: 'storm'` finds grow while one blows. **Conditions** (`conditionsAt(data, wall)`, data under `conditions` in `content/items.json`): the day is `dayIndex(wall)` (the one `weatherAt` uses; it starts at dawn), the week `weekIndex(wall)` (Monday 00:00 UTC, always a dawn). A day's dice are `seeded(dayIndex * 7919 + seed)` (mulberry32): one daily condition by `weight`, a second when a roll is under `second`, never of the same `group`; the week's is `weekly[weekIndex % length]`, next week's the one after. Nothing is stored. The World's `tick` notices a new day or week, tells everyone online (`conditions`, and the welcome has them), opens and closes find rules with a `condition` (the same path as `when`; a daily condition's finds are sown afresh every dawn, and the NAPO cache's `respawn` of a day means a picked one does not come back), gives each map's watchers the lairs of a condition that moves them (precomputed with `TileMap.lairs`, and awake ones outside them are sent away) or none while one puts them to sleep, and for `fireOut` douses one untended fire of the map or its shelters (`Fires.douse`), picked with `seeded(dayIndex)`. The first tick after start-up does all of it but put a fire out, so a restart mid-day cannot put one out again. The client draws a `fog` condition by capping the fog outdoors on that map (`Game.fogCap`, `view/world.ts`), and Mira's first line and the dawn banner come from the names. For play-tests, `CLOCK_SHIFT_MS` moves the world's clock (added to `epochOffset`; refused when `NODE_ENV=production`), and `PARCEL_DAY_MS` shortens the parcels' days (Home, below). Flashes are not on a clock: every `flashes.every` seconds the World picks someone out in the open within the rule's `steps` from home and starts one on a tile around them (`flash`, with `left` seconds: it glows, then discharges in its last `FLASH_BURST_S`).
- **Tall grass** (tile `h`, kind `tallgrass`) hides whoever stands in it from creatures, and only from them. The rule lives in one place, `hidden(map, x, y)` in `packages/shared/src/map.ts`, and everything creatures decide goes through it: `TileMap.creatureMayStand` (walkable, not an exit, out of the light, away from fires, not hidden) is where any creature may stand, step or wake, so `lairs`, the watchers' steps and the skulkers' paths never enter it; and the World notices only who is `noticeable` (`exposed`, and not hidden), so watchers never come for someone in it, skulkers neither hear nor see them, and a chase ends when the prey reaches it (`giveUp`, as when a skulker loses someone). `exposed` alone still starts flashes, and nothing else looks at tall grass: the drain, wetness, hitchhikers, surges and storms find you there as anywhere. A watcher faced from tall grass still freezes: `faces` is about who looks, not who is seen.
- **Watchers** (a map's `watchers` rule): server-side creatures that wake on tiles of their rule far from every player, step every `WATCHER_STEP_MS` toward the nearest noticeable player within reach along a short breadth-first path (never onto light, fire, exits, tall grass or near a flare), and freeze while any player within sight faces them (`faces`). Someone carrying a live find is prey from `WATCHER_HUNT_LIVE` (12) steps instead of `WATCHER_HUNT` (9); someone glowing with an afterglow (below: Pieces) is nobody's prey (`glowing`). Reaching someone: `touched` (energy and one random unit of the bag lost), and away for a while. The map hears `creature` and `creatureGone`.
- **Skulkers** (a map's `skulkers` rule): wake like watchers, on fern tiles of their range, but only while their rule's time holds (`night`: night and aurora; `storm`: the region's storm phase), and sink away (`creatureGone`) when it ends. One lies still in its lair until a noticeable player is within `SKULKER_HEAR` walking (`SKULKER_HEAR_HUSHED` when their gear has the hush quirk: `heardFrom`) (their last step ended under `SKULKER_HEAR_MS` ago) or `SKULKER_SEE` standing, and a path to them exists inside its range; then it steps every `SKULKER_STEP_MS` (slower than `STEP_MS`) along `pathStep` over tiles a watcher may stand on and within its steps from home, and its `creature` messages carry `chasing` (the player's id; clients rustle the ferns, play the cry and the chase loop). It gives up after `SKULKER_CHASE_MS`, when the prey is no longer noticeable (in light, by a fire, near a flare or in tall grass) or on its map, or when no way inside its range is left, notices nobody for `SKULKER_CALM_MS` and walks back to its lair (a full-map search, so it always finds the way). Catching someone: `touched` with `by: 'skulker'`, `SKULKER_CATCH` energy, and one random bag slot dropped as the player's pile (`dropBag` with a slot: no echo, replacing an older pile), then away like a watcher; a catch that empties the bar is a collapse.
- **Hitchhikers, flares, marks**: a hitchhiker is a flag on the player, rolled each tick in the dark, deep in (`hitch` tells them). A flare (`use`) lasts `use.flare` seconds on its tile (`flare` to the map); the client gives the nearest one a real light of its own, always in the scene so the shader never changes. Marks (`use` of an item with `use.mark`) are records like piles: saved in their own table (`marks`), at most `MARKS_PER_PLAYER` each, each fading at its own `until` (`markLifetime(mods)`: `MARK_LIFETIME_MS`, a day, times the painter's `Mods.marks`, which Good neighbor raises; `marks.fades_at`, migration `015_thanks.sql`, null for a mark of an older release, which fades a day after `placed_at`). The `did` of a mark and the client's question before painting one both say how long it shows, from the same rule. `MarkView` names its painter (`owner`, `name`), for thanks.
- **The Old Stone**: the first `stone` object in the world. Shards (`charge`) fed to it (`feed`, with a `count`: it takes all of them, from every slot holding that item) add up; at `STONE_NEED` it wakes, burns one every `STONE_SHARD_S` and sleeps at none. Everyone online hears `stone` (an Outgoing to `'all'`). Saved in `world_state` under `stone`, with the wall time of its charge, so it keeps burning across a restart.
- **Strange objects** (`use.identify`) turn into one of their `reveals` (by weight, `reveal`), only in town or a house whose door opens onto one. The fifteen and their weights are data (`content/items.json`, weights out of 120), and `packages/shared/test/strange.test.ts` holds them to the design's shares: rare gear 1 in 20 in all (an anomalous piece for each slot but the bag: the shard-lined cap and NAPO's crew kit), charms about 1 in 4. **Charms** (kind `charm`) change `Mods` while in the bag, like feats, one of each kind (`charmsIn`).
- **Feats** (`feats.ts`): one table, `FEATS`, gives each feat its count (`stat`), the value of `Mods` it changes and which way (`less`, `more` or a `chance`), and five ranks (`RANKS`), each the count that reaches it (`need`) and its share (`by`). A rank is never stored: `rankOf(feat, count)` follows from the counts, so the counts saved before ranks existed are worth their rank the moment a player joins, and nothing announces it. The server counts in `players.stats` (jsonb, so a new count needs no migration): `rainSteps`, `nightSteps`, `heavySteps` and `farSteps` in `move` (`stepCounts`: out in the wilds, in the rain, in the dark, with a bag at least `HEAVY_LOAD` heavy as it feels, `FAR_STEPS` or more from home), `fed` in `feed` (once for every unit a fire takes, however many one `feed` puts in), `mended` in `mend`, `found` in `pickFind` (finds out in the wilds or a shelter off them, never piles) and `thanked` when someone thanks them (below). `thanked` is the one count others add to, often while its owner is offline, so it lives apart: a column of its own, `players.thanked` (migration `015_thanks.sql`), which only `Storage.creditThanks` changes (`thanked = thanked + 1`, one statement, whoever is online), while the World keeps the online player's copy in `stats`; a save never writes it (`savedStats`), so no save made from an older copy of the player undoes a thanks, and the previous release never touches it. For what people say once (The story), it also counts `made` in `craft` (each piece of gear), `collapsed` in `fall` and `surged` in `tick` (once for each surge whose front is over someone out in the wilds, away from a street light: `inSurge`, remembered by map and round, `Online.surgedIn`); no feat has these, and they are saved and sent (`stats`) as they go up. `told`, which of those remarks were said, rides in the same counts. A count that reaches a rank (`count`) tells the player `feat` (id, rank and every count) once, since counts only go up, saves them at once and refreshes their rates. `modsOf(stats, charms)` makes one `Mods` of every rank reached and every charm carried: factors multiply (`wetting` in `wetRate`, `load` in `bagLoad`, `hitch` in the hitchhiker's roll, `warmth` by a fire, `wear` in `wearDown`, `farDrain` in `energyRate` on tiles `FAR_STEPS` or more from home, `marks` in `markLifetime`), chances add up as separate tries (`double`: `pickFind` rolls `rng` for it, never for a live find, and the second unit goes in only if it fits; `got` then says `double`), and amounts add (`AMOUNTS`: `markEnergy`, the energy a crushed glowcap gives back, which `use` adds as far as the bar has room and says in `did.lift`). ## Home: the stash, XP and levels

- **Your cabin** ([own-cabin](../roadmap/own-cabin.md)): the home room (`stonebrook-home`) is `private: true`, which `validateMap` allows only on a room with a chest. `copyFor` gives each player their own copy of a private room, keyed by their id (Zones), so whoever walks in through its door is alone in it, whatever copy their save named; its main copy stays empty. at your own it answers `doorstep`, the friends whose street (not yours) has a lot free (`social.ts` keeps who each player online is friends with at hand: `World.friends`). **Moving next to a friend** (`{t:'move', to}`, at your own door, once in `MOVE_EVERY_MS`, 10 s): refused unless they are a friend (`not_friends`), live on a street (`gone`) other than yours (`neighbors`) with a lot free (`street_full`); - The rules are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/progress.ts`: `store` (into a stash, with the XP it earns; a slot's piece goes in after the pieces of its kind), `takeOut`, `takePiece` (the `n`th piece of an item, counted as taken out), `usedUp`, `turnedInto` (what a strange object taken out turns into, a piece of gear too, is owed in its place, so it earns nothing brought back), `traded` (a trade between two players: of each item a side gave, as many as it had taken out and not brought back, at most as many as it gave, move from the giver's `out` to the receiver's, both counted from the stashes as they were, so what was taken out and traded earns nothing when either stashes it, and a fresh find given away earns its XP for whoever stashes it), the XP curve (`levelOf`, `xpFor`) and `maxEnergy(level)`. A stash is `{ items, out, pieces? }`: how many of each item lie in it, how many were taken out and not brought back (those earn nothing when they return; using one up forgets it), and its gear piece by piece. Every one of them keeps `pieces` as they are.

The World fills it in `join`, from the time since `lastSeenAt` on its own clock (`now + epochOffset`; with `CLOCK_SHIFT_MS` set, the shift counts as time away too, as it does for piles and marks), and stamps `lastSeenAt` in `leave`, so a record that comes straight back without storage (another tab takes over) counts no time away; every save still sets it (Saving). Gifts (parcels, what a lockbox held) never reach it, and what pays off `out` earns nothing (what comes back after being taken out, what came out of a crate), so there is nothing to double. `progress` carries the cup (`ProgressView.rested`, left out when empty) and `fromRest`, the part of `gained` the cup paid; the welcome's `progress` has the cup too, and `restedAway`, what the time away was worth (`restFor`, at most `RESTED_MAX`, whether the cup had room for it or not). ## The story

- The rules are pure functions in `packages/shared/src/story.ts`; the chapters are data (`content/story.json`, checked by `validateStory` against the maps and items: the people and desks by `id`, maps by id, items by id). A chapter has an `id`, a `title`, a `text` (what the journal keeps), one `when` (a `StoryEvent`: `store`, `reach` a map, `feed` a fire or the Old Stone, `pick` an item, `talk` to a person, `read` a desk) and `hints` by person. The first chapter has no `when`: everyone starts there.
- A player's place in the story is the id of the latest chapter they reached (`players.story`, migration `010_story.sql`; none: the first chapter). The World (`moveStory`) moves it on only when the event is what the next chapter waits for, one chapter at a time, saves the player at once and tells them `chapter`. The events come from what the World already does: storing, crossing an exit or a door, feeding a fire out in the wilds (a tended one refuses, and one in town never counts) or the Old Stone, picking a find, and `{t:'talk', x, y}`, which the client sends when it opens a person's lines or a desk's text next to it (the server finds who or what stands there). The client sends it as soon as its own last step is over, so when a slow network bunches up the steps before it and they still wait in the queue, the World hears it once they are walked, from where they took the player.
- Chapters are only ever added at the end, so an id the server's story does not have comes from a newer one (a release rolled back): that player is past every chapter it has, nothing moves them on, and their id is kept for when the newer story is back.
- The client bundles `content/story.json` like the items. The welcome carries `story` (its version, and the chapter you are in); a client with another version reloads. `storyLines` puts a person's hint in front of what they always say, or after it when talking to them reaches the next chapter; `journal` lists the chapters reached for the journal panel (`journal.ts`, `hud.ts`). A chapter reached is announced with a banner once no dialog, panel or fade is in the way.
- **One place for what people say:** `storyLines` is where a person's lines are put together, so whatever else comes to change them later (lines about what you did, or about the day) goes through it, in one order after the chapter's hint, rather than beside it. **Remarks** (`remarks` in `content/story.json`: who, after which count, the line) are what someone says once after the first time you did something: `remarksDue` gives those whose count (`collapsed`, `surged`, `made`, the `Milestone`s) reached 1 and are not `told` yet (a bit each, by their place, so remarks too are only ever added at the end; `MAX_REMARKS`), and `storyLines(story, chapter, npc, lines, stats)` puts them after the hint and before the rest. The client says them and keeps them told at once (`toldAfter`); the server keeps them told when it hears the `talk` (`remarked`), saved at once. Those counts reach the client as they go up (`count`, and every `zone`), so a remark heard on the same map is never kept told unsaid. The `talk` message is the one a client sends for people and desks, and for anything else read like a sign (the field notes, below); anything the server has to answer about them extends it. **One book:** the journal panel is the only one: the field notes are its second part, and later the notes found out there another.
- **Field notes** (`packages/shared/src/notebook.ts`; data: `content/notebook.json`, next to the story, checked by `validateNotebook` against the maps and items, by `npm run validate` and by the server as it loads it, `loadNotebook`, which runs without pages when the file is not there). A page has an `id`, an `area` (a town or wilds map, or `anywhere`), a `title`, a `text` and `when`: one `NotebookEvent` or a list of them, any of which opens it. An event is `{find: item, during?}` (a find picked up, or what a strange object turned out to be; `during` is `aurora` or `storm` when it was picked up on an aurora night or while its region stormed), `{read: id | {map, x, y}}` (a desk by its id, or anything read like a sign, `READABLE`: a sign, desk, paper, tag or stencil, by its first tile; `readableAt`, `readEvents`) or `{saw: Sight}` (`SIGHTS`, what the World notices). A page's `blanks` each have an `id`, an `ask`, a `fill` and one `when`. `notebookIndex` maps each event (`eventKey`) to what it may open and fill, and `noted` applies one event to a player's `NotebookState` (`{pages, blanks}`, ids in the order they came): the pages it opens, then the blanks it fills on pages open by then, or null, cheaply, when it does nothing. The validator also refuses a text that says where something is (`saysWhere`: the compass, a count of steps or tiles, a tile's two numbers; "the South Road" is a name). The World turns what it already does into events, in small hooks (`note`, `saw`, `found`): `pickFind` and `pickTool`; `use`, when a strange object is looked at (`looked`, and what it turned into); `heard`, for anything read (the client sends `talk` for signs, papers, tags and the jeep's stencil too: only the tile, never what it says); `hitch` and `unhitch`; `startFlashes` (whoever is within `FLASH_NEAR`); a skulker's chase starting (`rustle`, for whoever is within `RUSTLE_HEARD`) and ending without a catch in `giveUp` or `sendAway` (`escaped`, unless its prey collapsed during it, `Online.fellAt`; `grass` when they are in tall grass); `fadeLive`; the Old Stone waking (`woke`, for everyone online); and each tick `notice`: a surge whose front has passed a player under a street light or the door of their room (`surge`; `lit` when they reached the light after it caught them this round, `surgeRound`), a storm over them out in the open (`storm`) or over their room (`roof`), an aurora night in the wilds (`aurora`) and a pole within `HUM_NEAR` then (`hum`, in town too), a flash discharging on their tile (`burst`), an awake watcher within `SEEN_TILES` (`watcher`, and `froze` when they face it) and, crouched in tall grass, something awake on their map that would come for them otherwise (`hidden`). What opens or fills in is saved at once (`players.notebook`, jsonb, migration `021_notebook.sql`; a save without it keeps what was saved, as the release before, which never writes the column, does) and heard as `{t:'page', id}` and `{t:'blank', id}`; the welcome carries `notebook` (the version of content/notebook.json and the player's pages and blanks: a client with another version reloads, and a client facing a server from before it plays on with none). Ids a newer notebook wrote stay saved and never show. The client bundles notebook.json like the story (`Game.notebook`, `Game.fieldNotes`); the journal panel has two parts under tabs (`hud.ts`, `JournalTab`), and `fieldNotesView` and `fieldNotesHtml` (`journal.ts`, tested without a page) make the field notes: each area in the order its pages first name it, headed by `areaHeading` ("The Near Woods: 5 of 12"), with its open pages in the notebook's order, each blank as its question or its answer, and the pages opened since they were last looked at marked (`Game.freshPages`, cleared when the journal leaves them). The notebook button in the bag's header opens the journal there (`toggleJournal(true, 'field')`); from the menu it opens where the news is. A page's or a blank's banner waits like a chapter's (`newsBanner`, a `News` of kind `page` or `blank`), with a dot on the menu, the tab and the notebook until they are looked at. No new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`: older clients leave the new messages and the welcome's new field alone.
- **Notes people left and keepsakes** (`packages/shared/src/notes.ts`; the notes' words in `tools/notes-left.ts`, laid by the generators; the keepsakes in `content/items.json`): a note is a map object `note` (`id`, `by`: `NOTE_AUTHORS`, `name`, `text`, and `when`: `NOTE_WHEN`, with `faint`, what shows at another time), lying on the tile of a thing it may lie on (`NOTE_ON`: a table, shelf, crate, bed, pole, car, truck or luggage). It is `DECOR`, so it blocks nothing itself; `validateMap` checks what it lies on, that somebody can stand beside it, its words and that a rain note lies out of doors, and `validateWorld` that no two notes share an id. Each generator lays its notes last on its map (after the crates), so nothing that was there moves. The client reads one like a sign (`Talker.note`): `noteLines(note, weather, storm)` says its text, or at another time only `faint` (or `FAINT`), `noteShows` deciding (night: night or aurora; rain: rain, or a storm over the map; aurora); `Game.weather` follows the server's. As for any sign, only `{t:'talk', x, y}` goes; the World (`heard`, then `readNote`) checks the note shows now by its own sky and storm clock, and gives `NOTE_XP` (5) the first time only, without the play-test multiple or the rest (it is not stashing), saved at once (`players.notes`, jsonb, a list of ids, migration `022_notes.sql`) and heard as `{t:'noteRead', id}` with a `progress`. The welcome carries `notes` (ids read) and `keepsakes` (item ids home). A keepsake is an item of kind `keepsake` (stack 1, never used, burned, fed, grown, made, given in a parcel or paid with: `validateItems`), and `keepsakes: {energy, places}` says where each lies (one place each, on a walkable tile that is not an exit). It lies for each player alone: the welcome's and every `zone`'s `finds` add the player's own (`World.findsFor`: each keepsake on that map they neither carry nor brought home), with an id below zero (`keepsakeFindId`), so a client handles it as any find, and shared finds never grow on those tiles. `pick` takes it after a pile and a shared find (`findGone` to that player only). At the chest `store` brings keepsakes home apart from the stash (`players.keepsakes`, the same migration): their XP once, `{t:'keepsake', item}` each, and `maxOf` adds `keepsakes.energy` once every one is home (`keepsakeEnergy`), which the next `energy` says. A keepsake never falls into a pile (`dropBag` leaves it out, and with nothing else in the bag makes no pile), a crate refuses it (`keepsake`), a trade never takes one (`tradeable`), and thrown away it goes back: `keepsakeBack` sends `find` when the player is on its map. Joining keeps one of each in the bag, and none that is home (`oneOfEach`). The client draws a note on what it lies on (`noteModel`, interior.ts), a keepsake as a find (loot.ts, in gold), its icon in the bag (icons.ts), and the journal's third part, Notes (`notesView`, `notesHtml` in journal.ts; `JournalTab` `notes`), by writer in `NOTE_AUTHORS`' order with where each note lies, then the keepsakes home. No new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`: older clients leave the new messages and the welcome's new fields alone, and new maps and items make them reload anyway.

The client (`Game.firsts`) turns a new one into a one-line banner that waits like the others (`News` of kind `first`, "You are the first..." for the finder, by name), and the journal's Notes puts `firstInJournal` under each note and keepsake it has (`notesView`'s `firsts`).

## Equipment and the workbench

- The rules are in `packages/shared/src/gear.ts`: the six slots, the five elements, `resistOf` (what the pieces worn resist, each at most `RESIST_MAX`), `bagSlotsOf`, `gearEnergy` and `canMake`. Gear is an item of kind `gear` in `content/items.json`, with its `slot`, `tier`, `resist`, `bag` (a bag's slots), `bonus` (energy) and `color` (on your character); `recipes` say what the workbench makes from what.
- A player's gear is `players.gear` (`{ slot: item }`), null for one who never chose: they wear `STARTER_GEAR`, filled in when they join. The World keeps each online player's bar (level plus gear) and bag size, and feeds `resistOf` into `energyRate` (cold, electricity, radiation) and `wetRate` (wind).
- `{t:'equip', x, y, item}` and `{t:'unequip', x, y, slot}` work next to a chest: the piece moves between the stash and the slot; everyone on the map hears `gear` with what the player wears now (and `PlayerView` carries it, so newcomers see it). What you wear counts as taken out: `equip` takes its piece with `takePiece` (`out` + 1), and what comes off into the stash goes in with `store`, paying `out` off first and earning XP only beyond it (a piece that never was home earns it then, with `progress`). Without this a piece could go on at the chest, come off into the bag and be stored for its XP again and again. The releases before counted nothing for what was worn, so the first time this release has a player, what they wear that earns XP is counted as taken out once (`PlayerRecord.wornOut`, a mark kept beside the counts in `players.stats`, no migration): a piece put on back then earned its XP when it was stashed, and earns none again coming off. `{t:'bench', x, y}` next to a map object `workbench` (the home room has one, beside its chest) answers `bench` with the stash; `{t:'craft', x, y, recipe}` pays from the stash, puts the result in it, and answers `bench` and `did` (`made`). A recipe whose result is a tool gives the tool instead (below), and is refused with `have_tool` before anything is paid when the player owns it already; the client never asks for one you have (its workbench card says "You have it", its button greyed).
- **Anywhere, from the bag:** `{t:'wear', slot}` puts on the piece in that bag slot, and what it replaces takes its slot in the bag (so there is always room); `{t:'doff', slot}` takes off what a gear slot wears into a new bag slot, if one is free (`bag_full`). Never the bag: wearing a bag from the bag is `bag_at_home` (the chest checks what you carry against a bag's size) and taking the bag off `keep_bag`; `not_gear` and `empty_slot` as elsewhere. Neither touches the stash or `out`, neither asks first (nothing is used up), and there is no `did`: the player hears `bag`, the map `gear`, and the player their bar, rates and load (`wearGear` without the stash).
- **Tools** (items of kind `tool`: the paper maps and the field radio, and later the bolt cutters, waders and the maps found farther out) are each player's own for good, in the order they got them: `players.tools` (migration `012_tools.sql`), null for a player who never got one of their own, who carries `STARTER_TOOLS` (`toolsOf`). One path gives a tool however it comes, `World.giveTool(player, item)`: the workbench (a recipe that makes one), a find (a find rule whose item is a tool: picked up, it goes to the player's tools, is gone for everyone like any find and grows back by its rule, counts for the forager and the story like any pick, and never comes up double; one the player owns already is refused with `have_tool` and stays for someone else), and later a parcel or a chapter. The first tool of a player's own writes the starter tools down with it. It saves the player at once and sends `tools` (the whole list); how the tool came is for the caller to say: a find floats with `got` (`from: 'tool'`, the "+ Radio" over their head), and the workbench says it in the text box with `did` (`made`), which the client words from the item's kind ("You make a radio. It is yours for good: its button is in your bag."). A tool never goes into the bag, a pile, the stash or a trade: it has no slot and no weight, and nothing takes it away (a save without the list keeps the saved one; an id the server does not know as a tool, a newer release's, stays saved and is left out of what the player hears). `npm run validate` wants every tool to have a `name`, a `text` and an `icon` from `TOOL_ICONS` (`map`, `radio`: the drawing on its button, which the client has, `icons.ts`), every starter tool to exist and be a tool, a recipe that makes a tool to make one, and nothing to be paid with, mended with or turn into a tool. A tool made at the workbench says what it does with its item's `about` line after where it went.
- **A tool that listens** (the field radio, `radio`, made from 2 wire and 1 scrap): its item's `senses` say which finds it hears (`finds`, each always or `when: 'aurora'`) and how far (`loud`, `faint`), and `npm run validate` wants them on a tool only, `faint` beyond `loud`, and items that exist (a warning for one that grows nowhere). It is the client's alone: nothing about it goes to the server, which only gave the tool. Its button in the bag's header switches it on and off (`toolViews` marks it `on`, `toolsHtml` draws it pressed with a lamp), and the browser keeps the switch (`napoland.radio` in localStorage, on unless turned off). `radio.ts` (plain logic, tested without a page) finds your radio among your tools (`radioOf`), where the finds it hears lie (`heardFinds`, again only when the map's finds or the weather change) and how far the nearest is each frame (`nearest`, over those few spots), and turns that into the crackle's level (`crackleAt`: 0.6 to 1 within `loud`, 0.3 down to 0.08 within `faint`, 0 past it) and the hum (`radioCrackle`, `radioHum`: the crackle only in the wilds, the hum everywhere, both silent while off or in a storm over your region). A frame makes nothing new for it: main.ts keeps two of the radio's scenes and fills them in turn, so last frame's stays for the soundscape to compare with. The soundscape plays them as the `radio` and `hum` loops, a `pulse` shot when your map's surge clock turns restless (the Tower's pulse; only regions with a surge clock have one), and `tune` and `click` when it is switched.
- **Pieces** (`gear.ts`: `Piece`, `pieceFactor`, `wearSeconds`, `mendCost`, `newPiece`): what a player wears is `players.worn` (`{ slot: { cond, quirk } }`, migration `009_worn.sql`); the stash keeps counting gear in `stash.items` as before, with its pieces in `stash.pieces`, so an older release still reads it, and `fitPieces` makes the pieces match the counts whenever a stash changes or a player joins (counts from before pieces become new pieces). The World wears worn pieces down in `advance` while the player is in the wilds (`wear` by tier in `content/items.json`, slower by the mender's rank: `Mods.wear`), feeds the condition into `resistOf` and `gearEnergy`, and repeats it in the body of every `energy` message (`worn`). `{t:'equip', ..., n}` picks the `n`th piece of an item in the stash; `{t:'mend', x, y, slot}` at a workbench pays `mend` (by tier) from the stash, counts once toward the mender, and answers `bench` and `did` (`mended`). Pieces in the bag and in the stash do not wear down: only what is worn does. Quirks (`QUIRKS`, named in `quirks` in `content/items.json`) are in `PlayerView.quirks` and the `gear` message; the client draws them (footprints and flickering lamps, `wilds.ts` and `world.ts`) and hums (`main.ts`) from the surge clock. **Hush** is the World's: skulkers hear its wearer walk from `SKULKER_HEAR_HUSHED` (4) instead of `SKULKER_HEAR` (6). **Lodestone** is the wearer's page's alone, like the radio, since it only needs the finds the client already has and tells nobody else anything: `lodestone.ts` (plain logic, tested without a page) says whether something the Old Stone takes (`charge`) lies within `LODESTONE_TILES` (`shardNear`), and `Lodestone.update` tugs as one comes near and again every `TUG_EVERY_MS` while one stays near, never more often; a tug is news (`tug`: no banner) that pulses the quirk's name in the status panel for `TUG_MS` (`Hud.tug`) and plays the `tug` shot, the same in both ears, so it never tells a way. **Afterglow** is the World's: each tick, a flash discharging within `AFTERGLOW_NEAR` tiles (straight line, like `FLASH_RADIUS`) of someone whose gear has it sets `Online.afterglowUntil` `AFTERGLOW_S` on, once for each flash (`Flash.glowed`), so another flash starts it again. Their zone (the copy of the map they are in) hears `{t:'afterglow', id, left}`, and `left: 0` when it fades; `PlayerView.afterglow` carries the seconds left (as of the last tick) to whoever arrives, and it goes with its wearer to another map. A collapse ends it (`fall`). The client lights the glowing one faintly (`Afterglows` in `view/characters.ts`), counting down from what it was told. None of the three needed a new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`: the new items' version already makes an older page reload.
- **Upgrades** (`gear.ts`: `UPGRADE_MAX`, `UPGRADE_STEP`, `upgradeFactor`, `upgradable`, `nextUpgrade`, `upgradeChance`; the costs are data, `upgrades` in `content/items.json`, +1 first, each `{ needs, chance? }`, checked by `validateItems`): a piece's level is `Piece.level` (none: +0), so it lives wherever the piece does, on you (`players.worn`), in the stash (`stash.pieces`), in the bag and in a pile, all jsonb: no migration, and the World checks it on join like the rest of a piece (`isPiece`, `cleanPiece`). `resistOf` multiplies what each piece resists by `upgradeFactor(level)` (1 + 0.05 a level) before the 75% cap on the sum, and `wearSeconds(def, wear, level)` lasts that much longer, which `wearDown` uses. `{t:'upgrade', x, y, of}` next to a workbench, `of` a `PieceAt` (`{from:'worn', slot}` or `{from:'stash', item, n}`, the stash's order), pays `nextUpgrade(level)` from the stash (`payFrom`) and rolls the World's `rng` against `chance` only where it can fail, so the dice are the tests' to set; either way it answers `bench` (and, for a worn piece that went up, its rates and body first) and `did` (`upgraded`). Refusals: `too_far`, `gone` (no such piece), `not_upgradable` (worn clothes, bags, what is not gear), `top_level`, `missing`. Mending costs the same at any level (`mendCost` goes by tier). The protocol stays at 20: the message is new and additive, and the items' version (18) reloads an older client anyway.
- The client draws gear by slot and color: an icon per slot tinted with the piece's color (`icons.ts`, `iconFor`), and the character's parts in those colors (`characters.ts`, `Look`), the bag bigger the more it holds.
- **Outfits** (`packages/shared/src/outfits.ts`): `OUTFITS` lists them (id, name, a line, the level that opens each), and `mayWear(outfit, level, signedIn)` is the one rule: signed in, and the level reached; `outfitsFor` and `outfitsOpening` follow from it. Nothing but the one worn is stored, `players.outfit` (migration `014_outfits.sql`, null for none), so an outfit is never granted and never lost. `{t:'outfit', x, y, outfit}` next to a chest puts one on, or takes it off (null): the World (`outfit`) refuses a guest (`sign_in_first`; on a server without sign-in nobody is one), a chest out of reach (`too_far`), an outfit it does not have (`gone`) and one the level has not reached (`locked`); otherwise it saves the player at once and tells the map `outfit` (id and outfit, the player too), and `PlayerView.outfit` tells newcomers. A saved outfit the player may not wear (from a newer release, or one who plays as a guest now) shows as none while they play, and stays saved: `PlayerRecord.outfit` is left out then, and a save without it keeps what was saved, while null (taken off) saves none. The client keeps everyone's in `Game.outfits`; `lookOf` makes an outfit's `Look` the outfit and the bag alone, and `characters.ts` (`OUTFIT_LOOKS`, `dressOf`) draws it instead of the gear, the pack over it. The wardrobe's tiles are `wardrobe.ts` (`wardrobeView`), an outfit's card is `details.ts` (a `DetailRef` of `from: 'outfit'`), and wearing asks nothing (`Game.wearOutfit`): nothing is used up. A level's banner names the outfits it opened (`News` of a level carries the level it came `from`), once the chest is closed, and the welcome parcel's names what signing in put in the wardrobe (`Game.newOutfits`: all the level opens, unless the player wears an outfit already), with its "and a NAPO work suit in your wardrobe" (`parcelBanner`).
- **Merits** (`packages/shared/src/merits.ts`): past level 20 every `MERIT_XP` (1,500) past `MERITS_FROM` (level 20's 10,830) earns a merit: `meritsOf(xp)`, `toNextMerit(xp)`, `meritsLeft(xp, spent)`. What merits buy is code, like the outfits: `PATTERNS` and `BADGES` (`MERIT_LOOKS`: id, kind, name, `noun` and `plural` for sentences, a line, `cost` in merits), `meritLookOf(id, kind?)`, `whyNotBuy(look, xp, merits, signedIn)` (`sign_in_first`, `owned`, `no_merits`) and `mayWearLook(id, kind, owned, signedIn)`. Earned merits are never stored; `PlayerRecord.meritsSpent`, `looks` (the ids bought, in order, a newer release's kept) and `pattern` and `badge` (the ones worn) are, as `players.merits_spent`, `looks`, `pattern` and `badge` (migration `019_merits.sql`). A save without the merits or the looks keeps them (`COALESCE`, like the tools), so nothing bought is ever lost; a pattern and a badge are saved like the outfit, null taking one off. `{t:'buy', x, y, look}` next to a chest spends a merit: the World (`buy`) refuses a guest (`sign_in_first`), a chest out of reach (`too_far`), a look it does not have (`gone`), one owned (`owned`) and one no merit is left for (`no_merits`); otherwise it saves the player at once, and they hear `merits` (`MeritsView`: `spent` and `owned`, the looks this release has) and `did` (`bought`, with the merits `left`). `{t:'pattern', x, y, pattern}` and `{t:'badge', x, y, badge}` (null: none) wear one (`adorn`), refusing `sign_in_first`, `too_far`, `gone` (and one of the other kind) and `not_owned`; the player's zone hears `pattern` or `badge` (at the chest in their own cabin, only they do), and `PlayerView.pattern` and `badge` tell everyone they come upon after, the town as they walk out. On join a worn look the player may not wear (not bought, a newer release's, a guest now) shows as none and stays saved, as an outfit does; the welcome carries `merits`. The client asks first (`Game.buyLook`, `buyQuestion`), says what it did or why not (`noMerit`, said.ts), and wears without asking (`Game.wearLook`); it keeps everyone's in `Game.patterns` and `Game.badges`, and `merit` News (a stash that crossed a merit) waits like a level's. The wardrobe's parts are `wardrobe.ts` (`WardrobePart`, `lookTiles`, the merits line from `meritText`), a look's card is `details.ts` (a `DetailRef` of `from: 'look'`, whose Buy keeps the card open behind the question, `cardPress`), the status panel's Merits row is `status.ts`. `lookOf` passes the pattern into the `Look`; `characters.ts` draws it (`PATTERN_LOOKS`, from the `Dress`: its `torso`, the jacket's body box or a cape's bell, `shadeOf` its cloth, parts on the body and on `armL` and `armR`), baked with the rest. A badge's drawing is `icons.ts` (`badgeIcon`), put before the name on a name tag (`TagView.badge`) and before your own at the top of the screen (`Hud.setBadge`).

## Friends and private messages

- Friends, requests, blocks, messages and reports involve players who are offline, so they are not in the World: `apps/server/src/social.ts` reads and writes storage for every action, and `net.ts` hands it each message (one player's actions run one after another) and says who is online where.
- Storage: `links` (`friend`, stored both ways; `request`, from who asked; `block`, from who blocks), `tells` (private messages, deleted when read), `reports`, and `players.requests_off` and `players.trades_off` (migration `020_trades_off.sql`).
- Client messages: `befriend` (by `id` or `name`; if they asked you first, you are friends at once), `answer`, `unfriend` (also takes back a request), `tell`, `read`, `block` (ends a friendship and any request either way), `report`, `requests` and `tradeRequests` (the settings: friend requests off, trade requests off) and `friends` (the list again). The server answers with `friends`: the whole list, with each online friend's map, and both settings (`requestsOff`, `tradesOff`). After the welcome come `friends` and `tells` (every unread message); each new message to someone online is a `tells` of one.
- Refusals (`refused`, whose `action` is the message's `t`): `unknown_player`, `requests_off` (also when they blocked you, so nobody learns who blocked them), `you_blocked`, `not_friends`, `too_many` (20 requests waiting, or 50 unread from you), `slow_down` (20 messages a minute), `guest` (they play as a guest: nobody can ask them yet). With sign-in, a guest is refused every one of these messages with `sign_in_first`, checked in `Social.handle` with what the session was when the message came in (a report needs a reporter who answers for it); blocking and reporting a guest works. Nothing among friends is sent to a guest (no list or messages after the welcome, nor when someone blocks them): a guest's `joined` only reads whom they block, so chat keeps them unheard.
- The client keeps the conversation for the session only (`friends.ts`, `Game.talks`); opening someone's card sends `read`. A report carries the last thing they wrote you as the reporter's `quote`.
- **Trades**, face to face between friends (`apps/server/src/trade.ts`; the rules in `packages/shared/src/trade.ts`, the stash's side in `progress.ts`): like the friends' actions a trade needs storage (are they friends, does the other take trade requests), but it lives in memory, between two players online, and changes their bags, which are the World's. `net.ts` hands every trade message to it in the same queue as the friends' actions (an ask reads storage, and what a player sends after it waits for it), and tells it what it sees going out: a `zone` (the player walked off the map, into another copy of it, or collapsed: off), a `bag` (their side keeps only what the bag still holds, `World.keptOf`, `keptOffer`), a player leaving (off); Social tells it when two players are friends no more (`unlinked`: off); and every tick it looks at each trade (`Trades.tick`): an ask waits `TRADE_ASK_MS` (30 s) for its answer, and two players farther apart than `TRADE_REACH` (10 tiles, center to center) or no longer in the zone they began in (the copy of the map they are in: `World.where`, so two players in two cabins never trade) are off. Client messages, all checked by zod: `tradeOpen {id}` (friends, signed in on a server with sign-in, in one zone and within reach, neither in a trade, the other taking trade requests; at most `TRADE_ASKS_PER_MINUTE`, 5, a `RollingLimit`; asked back, it opens at once), `tradeAnswer {id, yes}`, `tradeOffer {items: [{slot, count}]}` (the whole side as picks of the bag, which `World.offerOf` makes what the bag holds with `offerFrom`: each piece of gear and live find its own entry, with its piece or when it was picked, stacks by item and never more than carried, never a tool, a sealed thing or a keepsake: `tradeable`), `tradeReady {on}`, `tradeConfirm` and `tradeCancel`. Any change to a side takes both Readys and both Trades back. Both players hear `trade` (a `TradeView` from their side: whom with, `asking`, `asked` or `open`, what each side gives as `offerView` has it, without a live find's `since`, and both Readys and Trades) after every change, and `tradeOver` once (`done`, with what each gave and got, or `off`, why and by whom). When both have pressed Trade, `World.swap` does it in one step (`swapOffers`: each bag must still hold all it offers and have room, after what it gives, for all it gets, or nothing moves and the Trades go back, with `bag_full` to the one whose bag it is and `their_bag_full` to the other), `traded` moves the stashes' `out`, both hear their bags and rates, and net.ts saves the two players together (`Storage.saveTogether`, one transaction) before the bags go out, then `tradeOver`. Refusals (`refused`, `action` the message's `t`): `sign_in_first`, `unknown_player`, `not_friends`, `trades_off`, `busy` (the other is in a trade, or asked), `trading` (you are), `too_far`, `slow_down`, `nothing_to_trade`, `bag_full`, `their_bag_full`. The client (`trade.ts`, plain logic tested without a page; `Game` and the trade panel in `hud.ts`): a friend's ask is a question in the text box (`Ask.tag` lets the game take it back when the ask is over, and it waits for a question or someone's lines to end), your side runs ahead of the server's answer (`Game.tradeMine`, sent as picks of the bag: `tapSlot`, `stepRow`), the panel follows every `trade`, a trade called off by closing its panel (by hand: its X, B, the stick, a tap on the world, another panel) ignores what the server says of it until its `tradeOver`, and the text box says how it ended (`tradeOverText`) or why not (`tradeRefusal`). A friend's card has Trade, greyed out while they are not near (`tradeReach`, from the tiles the game knows).
- **Chat** (`apps/server/src/chat.ts`, rules in `packages/shared/src/chat.ts`): `{t:'say', to: 'world' | 'local', text}` from a signed-in player (`authSub`; anyone else, a guest among them, is refused with `sign_in_first`), at most 5 in any 10 s, goes out as `said` to everyone online, or to everyone on the speaker's map within `LOCAL_REACH` (12 tiles), the speaker included, but never to someone who blocks the speaker (`Social.blocks`, kept in memory for players online). Words in `content/words.json` (next to the items; `npm run validate` checks it) are masked by `maskWords`. Nothing said is stored or logged. The client keeps the last `CHAT_LOG` lines and draws local lines as bubbles over heads.
- **Calls without words** (`apps/server/src/calls.ts`, the kinds and numbers in `packages/shared/src/calls.ts`): `{t:'call', kind: 'here' | 'come' | 'thanks'}` from anyone, a guest too (there are no words to filter), at most one in any `CALL_EVERY_MS` (2 s: a `RollingLimit` of one; sooner is `refused` with `slow_down`), goes out as `{t:'called', id, kind, x, y}` to everyone on the caller's map within `CALL_REACH` (25 tiles, center to center), the caller included, from the tile the server has them on, but never to someone who blocks the caller (as in chat). Nothing is stored or logged. The client (`calls.ts`, plain logic) sings each one heard as a `call` shot of the soundscape: the caller's voice is one note of `VOICES`, a pentatonic scale over two octaves, picked by `voiceOf(id)` (FNV-1a mixed, so it has nothing to do with the jacket color); the call's shape comes from `CALL_SONGS`; `callPan` pans it by the direction it comes from (west left, east right, at most `PAN_MAX`), `callGain` quietens it with distance (down to `CALL_FAINTEST` at the reach) and `callTone` dulls it (a lowpass from 9 kHz down to 1.8 kHz). `sound.ts` builds its few nodes as it plays (a `PeriodicWave` voice with a slow vibrato and a breath of noise, a lowpass, a gain, a `StereoPannerNode`). `Game.calls` keeps each one heard for `CALL_NOTE_S`, and the HUD draws a note in the call's shape and the caller's jacket color over their head where they stand, only ever where they are on screen. A hidden tab runs no frames, so calls wait for it: `Game.takeNews` leaves out any heard more than `CALL_FRESH_MS` ago, and a crowd of old calls never sings at once when the tab comes back.

## Connecting and signing in

The server runs in one of three sign-in modes (`AUTH_MODE`, `apps/server/src/auth.ts`), and `GET /auth-config` tells the client which. It is never cached, so the page loaded after a release that changes the mode sees it.

| Mode | `/auth-config` | Who you are |
|---|---|---|
| `legacy` (the default) | `{mode:'legacy'}` | No sign-in: a name makes a character, and its token (kept in the browser's localStorage; only its SHA-256 is stored) logs back in. |
| `dev` | `{mode:'dev', providers}` | An email, believed without a code, so each tab can be someone else. For development and tests (`npm run dev` and the local `compose.yaml` set it); refused when `NODE_ENV=production` unless `ALLOW_DEV_AUTH=1`. The identity is `dev:` and the email in lower case. `providers` only shows the Google and Apple buttons, which say they need a Supabase project. |
| `supabase` | `{mode:'supabase', url, publishableKey, providers}` | Supabase Auth: the client (`supabase.ts`, supabase-js) has a 6-digit code emailed and checks it, or signs in with one of the `providers` (Google, Apple; below), and Supabase keeps the session in the browser and refreshes it. The server checks the access token (a JWT) with jose against the project's public keys (`<url>/auth/v1/.well-known/jwks.json`, kept 10 minutes and fetched again early for a key it has not seen; HS256 with `SUPABASE_JWT_SECRET` only for projects that still sign with a shared secret): issuer `<url>/auth/v1`, audience `authenticated`, not expired (30 s of clock difference allowed), not an anonymous user. The identity is the token's `sub`, Supabase's user id, however the player signed in. |

`providers` is `AUTH_PROVIDERS` (`google`, `apple`, in the order the card shows them; empty until the Supabase project has them set up). It comes over HTTP, and zod leaves out fields a client does not know, so a page from before it reads the answer as it always did, and a name this client does not know is left out: no new `PROTOCOL_VERSION`.

**Google and Apple.** The email card shows a button for each provider listed (`providers.ts`, inline SVG logos), above the email. A tap writes down in localStorage which provider it went to, when, and for which project (`napoland.signingInWith`), then `signInWithOAuth` leaves for the provider through Supabase with the PKCE flow (`flowType: 'pkce'`: supabase-js keeps a one-time verifier in localStorage, which alone turns the code that comes back into a session; the email code is checked directly either way). The client uses that flow for every sign-in, and never the implicit one, which takes a whole session from any address the page is opened at: with PKCE, a link that carries someone else's session in its fragment signs nobody in (and `withoutAnswer` takes it out of the address). The provider sends the player back to the game's address (`location.origin`, which the project's Redirect URLs allow): a new page, where supabase-js exchanges the code for the session as it starts and the client takes what is left of the answer out of the address (an error, or a code it could not use). `start()` reads the note and forgets it. With a session, the page plays as after a code: the hello carries the access token and the guest's token, and the claim, `has_character` or `need_name` follow unchanged. The guest's token goes with a signed-in hello only after a sign-in this page made itself (a code it checked, the provider it left for, in dev mode an email typed in it), until the welcome: a session found otherwise (kept from before, or one the page never asked for) plays the account's own character without it, and when the account has none (`need_name`) while the browser keeps a guest, the keep card asks first, "Sign in as <email> and keep this guest character?" (Keep it: the hello again with the token, the claim) or "Play as a guest instead" (signed out here, the guest plays on). Without one (cancelled, refused, a code that could not be used), the email card comes back with "Signing in with Google did not finish. Try again, or use your email." under the buttons, and its way back to the guest. A note older than 10 minutes, or from another project, is forgotten without a word. A page the browser brings back from its history as it left it (`pageshow`, `persisted`) goes the same way.

**Play first, sign in to keep it.** With sign-in (dev and supabase), whoever has not signed in plays as a **guest**: a character with no `auth_sub`, made and played by its token exactly like a character without sign-in (the token in the browser, only its SHA-256 on the server), in dev mode kept by the tab (sessionStorage) so each tab can be someone else. The welcome says `guest: true`; others see `guest` in the guest's `PlayerView`. Chat and everything among friends are refused to a guest on the server (`refused` with `sign_in_first`, `action` the message's `t`), and nobody can ask a guest to be friends (`refused` `guest`); a guest can be blocked and reported, and still hears chat. A guest gets no `friends` or `tells`. `GUEST_DAYS` (30) after they last played, a guest is deleted (Saving).

1. The client asks `/auth-config`, shows the matching card (`signin.ts`, a small state machine tested without a page: play, email (with Google and Apple above it), code, name, account), then opens `/ws` and sends `hello` with `v` (protocol version, now 26: trades, which an older page could not answer) and:
   - legacy: a saved `token`, or a `name` for a new character;
   - dev and supabase, signed in: `auth` (the email, or Supabase's access token, asked for afresh before every connection), the guest's `token` kept in this browser (or one from before sign-in) if there is one, only after a sign-in this page made (or its player said to keep the guest) and unless it was set aside for the account's own character, and a `name` once the server has asked for one;
   - dev and supabase, not signed in: the guest's `token`, or on a first visit a `name` from the play card, the first card (**Play now**, with "I have played before: sign in" under it).
2. With sign-in, the server gives the identity its character (`players.auth_sub`: one per identity). If it has one and the hello brings the token of a guest (a character nobody has claimed), the server answers `has_character` with the character's `name` instead of playing it: the client's account card asks "Play as <name>? Your guest character stays in this browser.", and says hello again without the token (it keeps it aside, `napoland.guestSetAside`, until you sign out), or signs out of the account in this browser and plays the guest on. Without a character of its own, the character of the hello's `token`, if nobody has claimed it yet, becomes theirs (`claimed: true`): that is how a guest, or a character made before sign-in, carries over, by signing in on the browser that made it. Without that, a new character with the `name` (the name rules and the limit of new players per address apply); without a name, `need_name`, and the client asks "Choose a name for your character". An `auth` that proves nothing (malformed, expired, forged, for another project) gets `sign_in_required`: the client refreshes its session once and reconnects, and after that asks to sign in again. If Supabase's keys cannot be fetched, the connection closes with 1011 and the client tries again later.
3. Without `auth` on a server with sign-in, the hello is a guest's. Its `token` plays its character, unless someone has signed in with it since: then `sign_in_required` (only they play it, signed in; the client forgets the token and shows the play card, saying the character is kept with the account), and an unknown token gets `unknown_token` (the play card). The server marks the guest seen (`players.last_seen_at`, only while nobody has signed in with it) before it plays, so the cleanup never takes a guest who is back. A claim of the same character that lands while such a hello is on its way wins, whenever it lands: the token hears `sign_in_required`, and the account's session, playing already or gone a moment ago, is never replaced by it nor turned back into a guest's. A `name` makes a new guest, held to the name rules and the limit of new players per address like anyone new. A hello with neither gets `sign_in_required`.
4. The server answers `welcome`: your id and name, the map you are on (id and version; a client with another version reloads; never the copy of it) with everyone, every find and every pile in your zone, `stepMs`, the weather, your energy and body, your bag, the fires, marks, creatures, flares, flashes, and surge and storm clocks of your map, the Old Stone, your feat counts, your tools (the items of kind `tool` you own, in the order you got them, kept apart from the bag, `toolsOf`: until you get one of your own they are `STARTER_TOOLS`, a map of every area there is: Stonebrook, the Near Woods and the South Road; see Equipment and the workbench; the bag's header shows every map you own as the one map button and each other tool as a button of its own, in the order you got them (`toolViews` in the client's `items.ts`; a tapped tool says what it is in the text box), and the map button, or M, opens the map whose `chart` is the area you are in (`mapFor` in `papermap.ts`: a room's area is the map its door opens onto, a street's the town its end leads back to) or says you have no map of this place; it draws a tool's `chart` once per map version in `papermap.ts`, never with anyone on it: the ground, water, roads, tall grass (a hatch of short strokes, and a key in the margin on a map that has any), cabins, NAPO's flat-roofed buildings and the mill's sawtooth, poles and wires, masts, fences, cars, trucks and the burned jeep by their size, signs, log decks, stumps, stakes, skids and a mark for each small thing left (luggage, boxes, the rocker, the piano, a bike, a birdcage, NAPO's pump and cages, mailboxes), and in handwriting the ways out, then the map's `places`, then the doors' names, each name moved a line up or down until it covers no other (a door's name that finds no room a line from its roof goes in front of its door; one named like a place is written once, as the place), so a new street of houses never moves a name that was there), the version of `content/items.json` and where you are in the story (the version of `content/story.json` and your chapter; another version of either also makes the client reload). Without sign-in (legacy) and for a guest it has your `token`; `guest` says whether you play as a guest, and `claimed: true` that this sign-in just made a guest, or a character from before sign-in, yours (the client says so: "Signed in. Your character is yours to keep."). Everyone on your map gets `join`.
5. Errors are explicit: `bad_version` makes the client reload, `unknown_token` shows the name card (legacy) or the play card (a guest's token), `bad_name` explains the problem, `need_name`, `sign_in_required` and `has_character` as above, `replaced` means the same player connected from another screen, `server_full` means try again soon.
6. Phones drop connections often; the client reconnects with backoff and says hello again (with a refreshed access token when it signs in). A connection the client closes on purpose (a guest leaving the world to sign in, a hello the server refused) puts the game offline at once, as a dropped one does (`Connection.stop` calls `onClose`), and a welcome for another character than the last, on the same map (the account's own after its guest), closes whatever the last one had open: the chest, the workbench, the text box.

The hello may be up to 9 KiB, since a Supabase token grows with the profile a provider such as Google adds; every other message stays under 1 KiB. The client prepares its hello before it opens the socket, so a token refresh never eats into the 5 s the server waits for it. Tokens and emails are never logged.

## Limits

No single client can wear the server down (`net.ts`, `limits.ts`):

- Each connection: messages of at most 1 KiB (the hello 9 KiB), 30 a second (bursts of 60), `hello` within 5 s, an answer to the heartbeat ping every 30 s. At most `MAX_PLAYERS` (500) online.
- Each player: 5 lines of chat in any 10 s (`chat.ts`), one call in any 2 s (`calls.ts`), 20 private messages a minute (`social.ts`), 5 asks to trade a minute (`trade.ts`), one knock at a neighbor's door in any 3 s and one move of their cabin in any 10 s (`world.ts`).
- Each address: `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_IP` (20) open connections; one more gets HTTP 429 before a WebSocket exists. `NEW_PLAYERS_PER_IP_PER_HOUR` (10) new players in any hour, new guests among them, counted in memory; one more gets `bad_name` ("Too many new players from your network"). Coming back to a character (with a token, with sign-in, or claiming one) is never limited.
- The address is the socket's. Behind our own proxy (Caddy), `TRUST_PROXY=1` makes it the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, the one the proxy added; the game port must then only be reachable through the proxy, or clients could claim any address. Addresses are never logged.

## Saving

Friends, requests, blocks, unread messages, reports and both settings are written at once (`social.ts`); trades are never stored, only what they did to the two players, who are saved together in one transaction the moment it is done (`Storage.saveTogether`: a swap never lands in one bag only). Players (position, map and the copy of it they are in, energy, bag, wetness, feat counts, XP, the cup of rest, stash, gear, the chapter of the story, their field notes, the notes they read and the keepsakes home, their tools, the parcels, the outfit, the merits spent and the looks bought and worn, the furniture in their cabin, until when they are cozy, and the street and lot their cabin stands on) live in memory while online and are written to PostgreSQL every 15 s, when they leave and on shutdown, and at once whenever items move between a bag and a pile (or a watcher takes one, a skulker makes one fall, a feat's rank is reached, a chapter reached, a page of the field notes opened or a blank filled in, a note read for the first time, a tool given, a parcel comes, an outfit, a pattern or a badge is put on or taken off, a merit is spent, furniture is made, or they are given a lot or move their cabin). The field notes (`players.notebook`, migration `021_notebook.sql`) stay null until a page opens, and so do the notes read and the keepsakes home (`players.notes` and `players.keepsakes`, migration `022_notes.sql`) until the first; a save that carries none keeps what was saved (`COALESCE`), like the tools. A player's furniture (`players.furniture`, migration `024_furniture.sql`) is kept like their tools: a save without it keeps what was saved; whether they are cozy (`players.cozy_until`) is said by every save, as the map is, and so is where their cabin stands (`players.street` and `players.lot`, migration `025_streets.sql`: both null until they first come home since streets came; the World loads every player's at start-up, `loadLots`, after the guests who stayed away are gone), and whether they keep their door to themselves (`players.door_off`, `026_door.sql`); that they read the letter about their street (`players.street_told`) stays once written. The first finders (`firsts`, migration `023_firsts.sql`: a secret, the player and the Zone's day, one row per secret, the player's row taking theirs with it) are written when they happen, never over one kept already (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`), and loaded at start-up with each finder's name. Saves of one player run one after another; a hello does not wait for them, so `net.ts` keeps what each player left with (`leftWith`) until all their saves are written, and someone who comes back meanwhile (a quick reconnect while the database is busy) plays as they left, with only whose character it is and the thanks received read from storage: otherwise what fell into their pile or went into a crate on the way out would be theirs twice. What a newer release saved that this one does not know (an item it has no definition of, in the bag, the stash or what was taken out of it, and a count it does not keep) is set aside as it was (`PlayerRecord.kept`, `cleanStash`, `cleanStats`), never shown or used, and written back with every save, like a newer release's tools: rolling back to this release and forward again loses none of it. A player's tools (`players.tools`, migration `012_tools.sql`) stay null until they get one of their own, which reads as the starter tools; a save that carries none keeps what was saved (`COALESCE`, like the chapter), so nothing takes a tool away, and the previous release, which never writes the column, leaves it alone. So does the outfit worn (`players.outfit`, migration `014_outfits.sql`), which a save writes only when the record carries it: null there is none, taken off; and so do the merits spent, the looks bought and the pattern and badge worn (migration `019_merits.sql`, Equipment and the workbench). A player's row also says whose it is (`auth_sub`, empty for a guest or a character made before sign-in until someone claims it), holds the hash of the token of a character made without sign-in, and says when they last played (`last_seen_at`, set by every save and when a guest comes back). With sign-in, a guest (no `auth_sub`) not seen for `GUEST_DAYS` (30) is deleted at start-up, before the piles and marks are loaded, and once a day after (`forgetGuests`), but only once the server has applied the rule for `GUEST_DAYS` (`guests_since` in `world_state`, stored the first time: the privacy policy promises to say such a change in the game before it takes effect, and characters from before sign-in count as guests). Their pile, marks, links, unread messages, thanks, what they left in crates and their first finds go with the row, a report about them stays without them; a partial index on `last_seen_at` for rows without `auth_sub`, migration `011_guests.sql`, keeps it cheap. A character someone signed in with is never deleted, and without sign-in (legacy) nothing is. Piles and marks are written on every change, each with the copy of its map it lies in (`zone`, `''` for the main copy), and loaded at start (the last hour's piles, the marks that have not faded; older ones are deleted), and so is the Old Stone (`world_state`). Who thanked whom (`thanks`, migration `015_thanks.sql`: one row per giver, helper and UTC day, both players' rows taking theirs with them) is written when a thanks is given and when its helper is told, loaded at start-up (the last 7 days'), and deleted once older than `THANKS_KEPT_DAYS`: at start-up and every hour (`forgetThanks`); each thanks received also adds one to the helper's `players.thanked` on its own (`creditThanks`). What lies in the crates (`cache_items`, migration `016_caches.sql`: one row a thing, whoever left it taking theirs with them, with the copy of the map its crate is in) is written whenever a thing is left or taken, and loaded at start-up; a thing whose crate is gone is forgotten. Finds, fires (and who fed them), creatures and flares live in memory only, so a copy of a map that closes and opens again has them afresh, as after a restart. Schema changes are SQL files in `apps/server/migrations`, applied in order at start-up and recorded in `schema_migrations`; they only add, so the previous release still runs if a new one rolls back.

## Rendering

three.js with low-poly geometry, flat faceted normals, a three-step toon ramp and back-face outlines. The terrain is one mesh built from the tile map (flat tops, vertical walls for ledges and banks), colored at every tile corner by `Ground` (`view/grass.ts`): low-frequency value noise seeded by the map's id, dry and damp patches, darker and more olive where the forest or a lone fir shades the ground, damp by water, a slight jitter per tile, and their own variation for asphalt (darker mended patches), lots and mud; the mesh blends the corners, so there is no checkerboard and a place looks the same on every visit (floors and walls indoors keep their own look). Trees (a tree object, or one per forest tile, varied by position), ferns and grass are instanced in blocks of 8x8 tiles with computed bounds so blocks off screen are skipped (the Near Woods has thousands of trees); rocks, mushrooms and road lines are instanced too. Grass is clumps of three-sided blades placed by tile position (`grassClumps`): one to three low tufts on a grass tile, about fourteen knee-high clumps on a tile of tall grass, one instanced mesh a block for each, their normals bent toward the sky so they are lit like the ground they grow from. One toon material sways them in the vertex shader (`GrassMaterial`, an `onBeforeCompile`): a gust across the map, harder in a storm, bending a blade more the higher it goes, and a parting around the four players nearest you. Time, wind and those four are uniforms that change every frame without a new shader, the session has one grass material (`sessionGrass`, kept when a view is freed) and so one grass program (`customProgramCacheKey`), and nothing about it is worked out per blade on the CPU. Whoever stands on tall grass crouches (`crouchToward`: lower, leaning forward, arms in, the flashlight with them); every client sees it, since it knows every player's tile. The props that never move (houses, cars, signs, lamps, poles, barrels, fences; NAPO's buildings, signs and masts from `view/napo.ts`; and what the town, the leavers and NAPO left out in the places: from `view/left.ts` the sawmill, the curtains and mailboxes of the houses whose people left, the cars of the jam, the logging truck, log decks, stumps and skids, luggage, boxes, the rocker, the piano, a bike and a birdcage, and from `view/napo.ts` the burned jeep, the box trucks, the pump, the sample cages and the survey stakes) are hundreds of little outlined boxes, so each map joins them into a few meshes (`bake` in `view/toon.ts`): one for every plain color (as vertex colors), one for all their outlines, and one for each material that glows (lit windows, lamp heads, car lights, NAPO's screens, the masts' red lights, which blink, and one faint glow shared by the rocks in NAPO's cages, `HUM`, the same day and night). The whole town's props then cost about six draw calls instead of a hundred and more; they are a few thousand triangles (tens of thousands with all that was left), cheaper drawn whole than split. Where each thing stands and how it turns are plain functions of its data (`vehiclePlace`, `deckLogs`, `curtainColor`), tested without a page. One car keeps the headlight, the spotlight moved onto it so the lights stay the same number: the one farthest from where you arrive (`headlightCar`), the old car at the end of the Near Woods' road. What moves (people, the Old Stone's crystal and rocks) stays separate. A person is baked the same way, one moving part at a time (`characters.ts`, `makePlayer`): the body, each leg and each arm is one mesh in the shared vertex-colored material and one outline, so a character costs ten draw calls whatever it wears, and an outfit's hat, cape or patches, and a jacket pattern's patches and bands (flat boxes a little proud of what they are on, turned and leaned with it where it slants), cost none. Street lamps and fires share four real lights, nearest first, handed over as you walk; the rest glow through their material, and the scene always holds the same lights (inside or out), so switching maps never recompiles shaders. Insides (`view/interior.ts`, `fire.ts`) are a plank floor, log walls (in NAPO's rooms, concrete slabs and courses of block; on the mill's floor, sawn boards) with the front row cut down to a baseboard so this camera sees in, furniture and a hearth whose fire burns the same in any weather; around a room everything is black, and rain, mist and wisps stay outside. A room without a fireplace is cold and dark (a cold hearth, `coldHearthModel`, has a hearth's stones and nothing that burns), its windows boarded, or curtained in the cloth its house shows the street (`roomCurtains`); a calendar or a drawing hangs on the face of the back wall above the floor it is read from. Outside, a house's doorway glows when its room has a fire, and its chimney smokes (`view/lighting.ts` decides how each kind of map looks in each weather). Finds and piles (`view/loot.ts`) are small glowing models, one instanced mesh per kind of item rebuilt only when the lists change, with a soft pool of light under each so they read as colored lights in the dark; your own pile has a ring in your jacket color. The world is drawn at up to twice the screen's CSS pixels; when frames stay slow (fewer than about 42 a second for 1.5 s of play) it is drawn a step coarser (`quality.ts`, down to half, never below one pixel a CSS pixel), and a step finer again once they come easily for 6 s; a step up that does not hold is tried again only after a minute, then two, then four, so the picture never flickers between two sharpnesses. A step coarser that has not made frames at least 10% faster after 1.5 s is taken back, and no other is tried until frames come easily again: that phone holds its frames to a pace (30 a second, saving its battery), and a coarser picture would only cost sharpness. One WebGL renderer lives for the whole session; each map gets a view that frees everything it made when you leave. The new map's view is built (and its shaders compiled, behind the black screen) before the old one is freed (`nextView`), and freeing a view keeps one of its materials for each program it drew with that nothing kept yet (`keepPrograms`, toon.ts): so arriving on a map compiles only what no map before it needed, and the programs kept are as many as there are kinds of material, whatever the number of arrivals. No shadow maps: round blob shadows under things, cheaper on phones and closer to the FireRed look. Weather (overcast, rain, night) changes lights, fog, rain and glow; at night your character carries a flashlight.

The camera keeps the same circle of world (radius 6.2 tiles) around you on every screen shape: it fits the short side, and a longer screen only adds view at the edges. On upright screens the view shifts so your character is above your thumbs.

## Sound

Every sound is made in the browser with the Web Audio API; nothing is downloaded. `soundscape.ts` is plain logic, tested without a page: from the game as it stands this frame (a `Scene`: the map, the weather, you, the fires, poles, creatures, flashes, the surge, what you carry, your radio, and the news that came this frame) and the scene a frame ago, it says how loud each loop should be and which one-shots to fire (a `Mix`). `sound.ts` builds every loop once, when the first tap or key lets sound start, silent, and each frame only eases their gains toward the mix (a loop whose sound changes with its level, the surge's or the radio's, retunes a parameter or two), so a frame makes no audio nodes; one-shots (steps, thunder, calls, the radio's pulse) make their few nodes as they play and let them go. The loops: rain, wind, the fire, the wires, the surge, a watcher, a skulker, the shimmer of a live find, the radio's crackle (a band of static and a looped buffer of clicks, played faster as it heats up) and its hum (low sine partials on D, beating slowly, with a slow swell). Everything goes through one master gain: the menu's volume, 0 when muted, and a muted game fires no one-shots.

## Interface

HTML over the canvas, sized with container query units from the screen's short side, so it scales from small phones to desktops and works in any orientation: joystick bottom left (four directions with a dead zone), A and B bottom right (B held opens the fan of calls, below), status top left (your name, the players here, the connection and a slim energy bar: red below 25%, pulsing below 10%, a bright tip while refilling; for a guest, a Guest label and a Sign in button under the name, a target of its own; a tap anywhere else on the panel opens the Status tab), one menu button top right (no minimap and no marker: the paper maps open from the bag's header and never show where you are), the text box and the bag where they never cover A and B. The bag shows its slots (eight in the backpack everyone starts with) with an icon per item (`icons.ts`, inline SVG) and a count (a piece of gear: how much of it is left, and a star for a quirk), then the Wearing row; a tap opens a card (below), and B backs out one step at a time. A picks up what lies on your tile or the one you face before it talks; tapping a find walks there and picks it up. Below 20% energy the screen's edges darken. Arriving on a map (logging in, an exit, a collapse) fades in from black under a banner with the map's name, or with what happened. The menu's About (`about.ts`) opens where the bag does: who makes and publishes the game, the source code first (the AGPL offers it to everyone who plays), the privacy policy, the legal notice, the licenses and the version the server runs; every sign-in card ends with the same links in small print. The fonts (Fredoka and Nunito, from `@fontsource-variable`) are bundled with the client, so the page loads nothing from other sites.

The chest has two tabs in its header, the stash (what came in parcels and the first goal at its top, then the bag, what you wear and the stash) and the wardrobe (`wardrobe.ts`), and opens on the stash; a level that opens an outfit puts a dot on the wardrobe's tab until it is looked at. The wardrobe has three tabs of its own, Outfits, Patterns and Badges (`Hud.showWardrobePart`), and opens on its outfits each time the chest opens; a merit earned puts a dot on the Patterns and Badges tabs, and on the wardrobe's, until they are looked at (`Hud.setMeritNews`). In the chest, at the workbench and in the bag a tap looks and a second step acts (`details.ts`, plain logic tested without a page): a tap opens the card of what it is on (a `DetailRef`: a slot of the bag, something in the stash, what a slot wears, a recipe, a mend, an upgrade, an outfit), which `detailView` builds from the game (`HudHandlers.details`, told which sheet: `DetailState.panel` is `home` at the chest and the workbench, where gear goes on from and off into the stash, and `bag` in the bag, where it goes on from and off into the bag), and the Hud draws it (`cardHtml`) and keeps it current as the game changes, closing it when what it is about is gone. Its button, A, or a second tap on the same thing within `DOUBLE_TAP_MS` (`DoubleTap`) does what it says (store, take, equip, unequip, wear from or take off into the bag, use, craft, mend, upgrade, open a lockbox, wear an outfit) and closes it (using asks first with it still open); a card may offer a second thing, `more` (Throw away for what you carry, Take it out for a piece in the stash), which only its own button does (`morePress`), never A or a double tap; the second tap may land on the card itself, which can open under the finger, and counts when it is within `DOUBLE_TAP_SLOP` of the first, so the list never moves before a double tap could come (a hidden piece is scrolled into sight after it). B closes the card before its panel. In portrait the card docks under the list, which scrolls above it; in landscape the panel grows toward A and B, never over them, and shows it beside the list. Making and mending leave the panel through one function (`Hud.bench`, which calls `craft`), where asking first wraps them (below): Make or Mend greyed out (the stash is short) shakes and keeps its card open, and still goes to the game, which says in the text box what the stash lacks and sends nothing. A piece's name comes from `pieceName`, with its level after it ("Raincoat +3"). The Wearing row is drawn twice, in the bag and at the chest, from `wornViews` through `wearSlotView`; a live find's card in the bag gets its countdown line from `tickLive`. The workbench's list (`benchHtml`) puts mending, upgrades (`upgradeViews`, one row a piece that can go higher, its id `upgradeId`) and recipes under their headings; an upgrade's card (`upgradeStats`) shows what the next level changes, whole, beside its condition, what it takes and how often it works. A piece's level shows in gold on its slot (`slotHtml`, `wearSlotView`).

**Asking first, and saying what it did.** One text box holds someone's lines, a question, or what it says by itself. The question is `ask.ts`, plain state tested without a page: `Question` (YES chosen to start, up and down choose, left and right count between `min` and `max`, a question whose words say how many is a function of the count) and `Repeat` (holding − or + or a side of the stick: a step at once, then after a moment faster and faster). Anything that uses something up asks through one call, `Game.ask({ who, text, count?: { min, max, start? }, yes(n), no? })`: feeding a fire (`tend`: the best fuel by the bag's slot rule, how many up to `fireTakes` and `FEED_MAX`) or the Old Stone (`offer`), `use` and `discard` from the bag, `craft`, `mend` and `upgrade` at the workbench (the one place all three are sent from: a card's button, A on the card and a double tap on a row all reach them through `Hud.bench`), opening a lockbox at the chest (`openSealed`); later features ask the same way. While it asks, the Game routes the stick, A, B and taps to it and nobody walks (`driveMe`); main.ts sends A and B to it before a card or any open panel, and the stick does not close the panel it was asked from. When the Game already knows it cannot happen it says why instead (`inform`). YES sends the message (to wherever that item lies now, `actOn`) and keeps the question up, `waiting`, until `did` or `refused` takes its place. What the box says by itself (`Game.note`) closes at its time (`noteMs`: about 4 seconds, longer for more to read), with any press (which then does nothing else) or when you walk; a note that comes while a question or someone's lines have the box waits for them (`later`). The words are `said.ts`, plain functions tested against the real items: the questions, why not, and `didText` from a `Did`, with each item's `noun` and `plural` from content/items.json ("3 resin", "a shard", "rubber gloves") and the `about` line of whatever a strange object turned into. hud.ts draws it (`setAsk`, `setNote`, told only when `Game.boxChanges` moves; `setDialog` every frame, writing only what changed): YES over NO in a frame at the right with a caret on the chosen one, − ×N + under the question, buttons at least a fingertip (`--hit`); for a note, a thin line along the bottom running out (one Web Animation per note). While it asks or says something, the box stands above the panels (the bag, the workbench) over a transparent scrim that takes a tap anywhere else (NO, or closing it), with the stick and A and B above the scrim; in landscape the stick hides as it does while someone talks, so touch answers in the box.

**B held, and the fan of calls.** B keeps the finger it went down with (pointer capture), so the finger can slide off it. `CallButton` (`calls.ts`, plain logic tested without a page) decides what B is: pressed with a panel, a card, the text box or a question open (or the sign-in cards, or the screen fading between maps), it is B at once, as it always was, so a hold never takes a B that would close or back out of something; otherwise a release within `HOLD_MS` (300) is a tap, B as ever (the bag, back), and a hold that long opens the fan, drawn in `.ab` from B's center (`FAN`: the three calls at 90°, 130° and 176°, `FAN_RADIUS` 1.7 of B's diameters out, each note `FAN_NOTE` 0.62 of it, a little more on the smallest phones, so the fan opens up and to the left, clear of A and of the stick, on every screen shape); a hold that something opened in front of before the fan came is B once let go. `fanChoice` reads the finger from B's center in B's diameters: on B it is here I am, out on the fan the call nearest in angle, off it (toward A, right or down) none, and letting go there sings nothing. A hold the system takes away (`pointercancel`) calls nothing. The fan says each call's words under its note until this browser has sung `CALL_WORDS_UNTIL` (5) calls (`napoland.calls` in localStorage); with a mouse and a keyboard (`hover: hover` and a fine pointer), each note also shows the digit that sings it while Q is held. `Game.call` sends it, or (within `CALL_EVERY_MS` and a little slack of the last) says "Catch your breath first" over your head instead.

On a computer the keyboard drives the same handlers as the stick and the buttons (`keys.ts`, plain logic tested without a page): WASD and the arrow keys by `KeyboardEvent.code`, E or Space for A, Q or Escape for B (Q held is B held: `holdB`, and 1, 2 or 3 meanwhile, by `code` on the row or the keypad, sing that call; Escape while Q is held, or the window losing focus, cancels it), Enter for the chat (in the chat's line Enter sends, and on an empty line, like Escape, closes it: `chatKey` in `hud.ts`), M for the map of where you are (by the letter it types, `KeyboardEvent.key`, since layouts move it: on a French one the key in its place types a comma; a layout without Latin letters goes by where it sits: `mapKey`). The keys held form a stack, the newest wins, and they are all let go when the window loses focus or the tab is hidden, so nobody keeps walking. The game leaves alone keys with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd, keys typed into fields, Enter and Space on a button reached with the keyboard (it presses itself; a button pressed by a tap or a click lets go of the focus at once, so Enter still opens the chat) and every key while the sign-in cards are up.

## Content

The world is data in `content/maps/*.json`: what the map is (town or wilds, depth), rows of tile letters (`t` is dense forest, `h` tall grass; `TILE_CHARS` in `map.ts` has them all), rows of height levels, exits to other maps, and a list of objects (trees, cabins, lamps, signs with their text, people with their lines and their colors, `look`; NAPO's buildings, warning signs, masts, `antenna`, and desks, `console`, which you read like a sign under their `name`; a fire in the open can have a `name`, which the notice board says; a crate for whoever comes next, `cache`, has the `name` a letter calls it by). What the town, the leavers and NAPO left are objects too: a cabin with `curtains`, the mill (a house of style `mill`), a sign as a `cardboard` one or a `mailbox`; cars with their size (`w`, `h`), facing (`dir`), `paint` and a `door` or `trunk` left open, a `truck` (the logging truck, or NAPO's with style `napo`), NAPO's burned `jeep`, whose stencil you read from beside it; `logs` (a deck), `stump`, `skid`, `luggage`, `boxes`, `rocker`, `piano`, `bike`, `birdcage`; NAPO's `pump`, `cage` (its tag read like a sign) and `stake`; in rooms a cold `hearth`, `sheeted` furniture, a `crib`, a `clock`, a `paper` to read like a sign under its `name` (a note or a list on a table, or a calendar or a drawing hung on a wall tile, read from the floor below it), and the mill's `saw`, `carriage` and `sawdust`. `footprint` in `map.ts` says how many tiles each covers; stakes, skids and sawdust are only drawn, the rest stands in the way. A map can also list its `places`, the names people call spots by (the ring of stones, the sinks), which its paper map writes in. The maps are generated from fixed seeds (`npm run gen:map` for Stonebrook, `npm run gen:woods` for the Near Woods, `npm run gen:south` for the South Road; `npm run gen:street` draws Residents' Lane with no dice at all, after `gen:map`, whose door onto it it checks), so the world never reshuffles and players can share routes; `gen:woods` sows its tall grass last, from hand-picked tiles and only onto grass nothing else uses (off the ways, out of the lights, clear of doors, signs, poles and named places), so adding it moved nothing else; each generator then adds what the town, the leavers and NAPO left, last again: in the wilds only on forest it clears, as dead ends off the ways, checked as it goes so that only forest changes and every tile anyone could walk on stays walkable and as far from home; in town only on open grass off the roads and the tiles in front of what you read, cutting nobody off; its objects, exits and places after the old ones, in their order. `tools/test/content.test.ts` checks that against the maps as they were before (`tools/test/fixtures/before-richer-places.json`); the crates come after even that, by `gen:south` at the edge of the leavers' camp (on forest it clears, like what the leavers and NAPO left) and at the end of each shelter's list in `gen:interiors`; the rooms inside buildings are drawn by hand in `tools/gen-interiors.ts` (`npm run gen:interiors`, run after the others: it checks that every door leads into its room and back out in front of it), the home among them, with `private` and its `wake` point, and every lot's door on the street into it. `npm run gen` runs all five in that order (`gen:map`, `gen:woods`, `gen:south`, `gen:street`, `gen:interiors`). `npm run validate` checks every map on its own (its id, lowercase words joined by hyphens, sizes, tile letters, overlapping objects, a walkable spawn, reachable tiles, exits on walkable tiles, a way home in the wilds, signs, people, papers and cage tags you can stand in front of and jeeps you can stand beside, vehicles one tile across with their nose along them, the styles of buildings, rooms and signs and the mill's size, curtains only where nobody lives, a paper on a wall only if it hangs there, tall grass you can walk into that is never an exit or in front of a door, sign or person, and a warning where it could hide nobody) and then how they fit together (every exit leads to a real map, onto walkable ground that is not another exit, and every map can be reached from town).

## Running it

- **Development:** `npm run dev` (server in memory on :8080, Vite on :5173 with hot reload, reachable from phones on the same Wi-Fi). Sign-in is dev mode: any email, no code.
- **Docker:** `docker compose up -d --build` builds one small image (Node + bundled server + built client + content + migrations) next to PostgreSQL 17. Settings in `.env`. Sign-in is dev mode here too (`AUTH_MODE` in `.env` changes it).
- **Sign-in:** `AUTH_MODE` is `legacy` (the default), `dev`, or `supabase` with `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` (both public; `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` is the older name). `AUTH_PROVIDERS` (`google,apple`, or one of them) lists the providers the sign-in card offers besides the email code, once the Supabase project has them set up; a name it does not know stops the server, and in dev mode it only shows their buttons (`AUTH_PROVIDERS=google,apple npm run dev` to see them). Production runs `supabase`; what the Supabase project needs is in [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md).
- **Checking a server:** `/health` answers `ok`, the players online, the uptime and `version` (from `APP_VERSION`, default `dev`), so a deploy can be checked from outside. The About panel shows that `version` too, linked to exactly that commit's code when it is one.
- **Maps:** the server loads every map in `MAPS_DIR` (default `content/maps`) and refuses to start if any fails validation; `HOME_MAP` (default `stonebrook`) is the home town: new players start, and collapsed players wake up, in the home off it (the room with a `wake` point, each player in their own copy of it), or at the town's spawn when it has none; `ITEMS_FILE` (default `content/items.json`) holds the items and find rules, checked against the maps at start; `STORY_FILE` (default `content/story.json`) holds the story's chapters, checked against the maps and items, and `notebook.json` beside it the pages of the field notes, checked the same way.

## Growing later

The design's layers (your cabin, your street, the town square, the wilds) map onto the same server code: a zone is a map plus a copy key, players only hear about their own zone, and a map can already have copies (Zones). What each layer needs is a policy for which copy a player walks into, in the one place that decides it (`World.copyFor`): a cabin of your own is keyed by you (built: Home), a street by its number (built: Your street), and a crowded town square or region opens a numbered copy and keeps friends together (built: Crowds). When one process is not enough, zones move to separate processes (cabins and streets start on demand, the town square and busy regions get copies), with a small message bus between them for chat, parties and cross-zone presence; `zone` messages already tell the client everything it needs when it changes maps, whichever copy it lands in.

Production (one AWS server with Caddy, CI on every push, automatic releases with rollback, nightly backups) is described in [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md). Still to come: a scheduled bot that signs in with a saved token, plays a little and reports when something breaks.
